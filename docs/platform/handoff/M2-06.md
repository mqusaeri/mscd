# M2-06 handoff: onboarding, accounts and operator server

M2-06 builds the server side of onboarding a new customer: the organisation settings and the onboarding checklist of the Tenant Admin, the account routes of every signed-in user (pending memberships, decline, security overview, new recovery codes, read-all), the invitation of an existing active account, the operator routes of multi tenancy (organisation list and create, factor reset), the seeding of applicability entries when a system is created, the daily jobs after a time zone change, the operator commands of the CLI, the slug confirmation of `install.sh` and the optional admin Job of the Helm chart. It writes the platform tables only in platform scope (M2-02 follow-up 9).

## Deviations

1. **`sign_in` is done when someone signed in through an enabled provider.** The connection test of `admin.idp.test` stores no result, so the step uses `user_identity.last_login_at` of a linked identity as the proof that the provider works. Without one it stays `optional` with the count of enabled providers.
2. **`local_login` is done once an administrator set the policy.** A policy other than `allowed`, or any `org.settings_updated` entry, marks it done; setting it back to `allowed` keeps it done. The default needs no action, so the step is `optional`.
3. **`repository` counts the Managed Vault of a new organisation** when the installation enables `managed_vault`; another repository counts once a connection check recorded it (`last_checked_at`) and its status is `ok`. A fresh organisation therefore starts with this step done.
4. **The email to an existing active account links to the app root.** The account accepts or declines in the app after signing in; a deep link waits for the URL routing of D2-14.
5. **`ops.users.reset-mfa` repeats the steps of `MfaService.resetFactor`** in `ops/operator-accounts.service.ts`, because that method has no reason parameter and the identity service is not owned here. The two must stay alike (see the follow-ups).
6. **`admin revoke-operator` ends every session of the account** with reason `roles_changed`, the reason of a role change in `SessionRevokeReason`; its memberships stay.
7. **`ops.organizations.create` only checks `preset`; M2-S09 applies it.** The route refuses a preset that `license.plans` does not name; the entitlement row is M2-S09 (D.2).
8. **Recovery code refusals are 422 `business-rule`,** with the message keys of enrolment, because a 401 would end the session in the web app; the route lists 409, 422 and 429.
9. **`RecoveryCodesService` takes `TenantScope` and `Clock` as optional,** like `IdentityInfra`, so an application without `TenancyModule` (the HTTP unit tests) still starts.
Deviations 10 and 11 of the earlier rounds (ticks plan their slot; a time zone change applies from the next whole UTC hour) are withdrawn: the run ledger of the orchestrator redesign replaced the design they described.

| Finding | Change |

| Minor: a time zone change at the slot boundary was still planned wrongly. A tick that starts a little early, before a change dated at or before its slot, used the old zone, and a change that committed after the tick read the zone and the trail counted at the slot all the same. A tick could also read the new zone while the trail query (`occurred_at <= now`) dropped the change. The next tick then replayed the slot with a zone and catch-up that the slot did not have, so the jobs of a local date were lost (east) or one ran twice (west). The sentence "a change that commits during a tick is either in the trail or applies from the next tick" was not true | `jobs/scheduler-tick.job.ts`: a change applies from the first slot whose UTC hour starts after it. The zone of a slot is the zone after the changes made before minute 0 of the slot's hour (`zoneOfSlot`, in place of `zoneAt`). The catch-up counts the changes from minute 0 of the previous tick's slot's hour to minute 0 of this slot's hour. A tick of a slot always starts after minute 0 of its hour, so every tick of the slot and every later replay of it give the slot the same zone and catch-up, whenever in the hour the tick starts and whatever it sees of later changes. `zoneHistories` reads the zones with `FOR KEY SHARE` on the organisation rows: `PATCH /admin/organization` locks its row with `FOR UPDATE` before it writes, so the read waits for a change that holds its row, and a change that locks its row later waits for the read. ## Orchestrator redesign: daily job ledger

An independent review after wave 2a found the replay design of the daily jobs unsound. Jobs read "today" when they start, so a job planned before a time zone change and started after it worked on another date (one date twice, another never; `documents.review-alerts` matches exact dates, so alerts were lost). A move east of 17 hours or more late in the day lost that date's jobs, a clock set back one hour enqueued a slot twice, a slot without a tick was replayed as if it had run, two ticks at the end of the 54 hour window could take different paths, the zone history was ordered by transaction start, the tick's key share scan and the PATCH's row lock could deadlock with an auditor access grant, and the PATCH had no `If-Match`. The orchestrator replaced the design with a run ledger (decision between waves 2a and 2b, recorded under R-14). This section replaces deviations 10 and 11 and the replay description of the earlier rounds.

### Additions to the decision

1. **One run per job and tick, the earliest date first, and none while a run of that job waits for its start** (fix round 1 widens this to a run enqueued in the last 30 minutes). `readiness.recompute`, `drift.evaluate` and `cycle.derive` have the E.2 key `<prefix>:<org>:all`, and a queue keeps one waiting job per key (E.1). Two dates of such a job sent in one tick, or one sent while a delayed run of the job waits, lose the later run: its ledger row exists and its send is dropped. A scratch run of the simulation without these two rules lost 90 runs in 2,000. The rule also keeps a job that records a state (`license.check`) from recording an older date after a newer one. The later date follows at the next tick, while it is still the current or previous local date: a move east of about 24 hours, or another outage of a day, before that tick moves it out of the window and its runs are lost (see fix round 2 of the redesign).
2. **`demo.fixed_today` wins over the payload date** (demo and test installs fix today everywhere). The ledger tests run without it.
3. **A send that the queue drops because a job with the same key already waits** (replaced in fix round 1): for a key with a date that job is this run, so the row stays; for an `all` key the tick deletes the row it inserted, and a later tick sends the run as a catch-up, unless the drop came at the last tick before the run's date leaves the window of the current and previous local date, or the row would have been the organisation's first on the ledger's first local date (see fix round 2 of the redesign).
4. **The tick cleans the ledger** (rows more than 14 days old), so the result of `cleanup.expired` keeps its shape.
5. **The planner tests and the simulation are unit tests** (`apps/server/test/unit/jobs/daily-run-planner.test.ts` with `daily-run-simulation.ts`), so `npm test` runs them without Docker; the database tests are in `test/int/onboarding/organization.test.ts`.

Tests changed and added:

- Planner, fresh ledger: with its only row at 00:05, the tick of 02:05 catches up the missing runs after that row and none before it or of the previous date; a ledger that starts at 00:20 does not catch up the runs of 00:05 and 00:15.
- Planner, the ledger's first day: a dropped `rr` send at 02:05 is caught up at 03:05; after a move 24 hours west, `cycle.derive` that the waiting rule skipped is caught up at the next tick; after a move 26 hours east, the skipped date is caught up although its runs were due before the first row. The test fails with either rule alone (checked by swapping the rule for a moment).
