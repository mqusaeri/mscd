# M3-02 handoff: organisation export

M3-02 builds D.4 of `docs/platform/MILESTONE-3.md`: the eight routes of the organisation export (six for Tenant Admins, two for platform operators), the four-eyes rule of H.2, the notices, the worker job `org-export.build` with its archive writer, the exclusion list with reasons, the expiry of exports in `cleanup.expired`, the commands `mscd-server export organization` and `export verify`, the coverage test of R-21, and the section "Export all data" of the on-premise guide.

## What was built

### Routes (`apps/server/src/modules/org-export/`)

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `org-export.service.ts` | `exports.organization.list`, `.create`, `.get`, `.decide`, `.manifest`, `.download` and `ops.exports.list`, `.create`. A request is `requested` when another active Tenant Admin exists, else `queued` with the message that nobody else approved it (H.2). The requester is refused with 422 `SOD-SELF-APPROVE`; a decided or expired request answers 409. One export flows at a time (409 `export_in_progress`: another export requested in the last 7 days, queued or building), and an organisation whose Managed Vault files exceed `lifecycle.export_max_bytes` is refused (409 `export_too_large`). Every request, decision and download writes its audit entry (`organization_export.requested`, `.decided`, `.downloaded`; `ops.export_requested` in the platform chain for the operator) and sends `organization_export_requested` to every active Tenant Admin and every primary Management System Manager. A download streams the part from the vault with `Content-Length`, `X-MSCD-SHA256` and the F.13 headers (`application/gzip`, the manifest `application/json`) and counts it. The operator routes answer 409 `single_tenancy` in single tenancy, 404 for an unknown slug, and 409 `export_not_available` for an organisation that is not active or offboarding. Exports are written in the organisation's tenant transaction; a status change switches the platform scope on for that statement (`inPlatformScope`), as the trigger of platform 0007 requires. Commands of one organisation are serialised with a transaction advisory lock. |
| `org-export.controller.ts`, `ops-exports.controller.ts` | The stubs of M3-01 replaced. |
| `people.ts`, `notices.ts` | Active Tenant Admins, notice recipients, names as the organisation shows them (handle of a member, pseudonym of an erased person, nobody for the system or an operator), and the notice texts in English and Indonesian. |

### Build (`org-export-build.job.ts`, `builder.ts`, `archive/`)

- `org-export.build` claims a `queued` row (also a `building` or `failed` row of an earlier attempt), deletes what an earlier attempt left in the vault, checks the size limit and the free space of the worker's temporary folder (`os.tmpdir()`, so `TMPDIR`), builds, uploads every closed part to `exports/organization/<exportId>/` of the organisation's Managed Vault (a `storage_object` row each, the SHA-256 the gateway computed compared with the part's), uploads the full manifest, and completes in one transaction: `ready` with parts (`{part, name, sizeBytes, sha256, storageObjectId}`), counts, manifest object and SHA-256, expiry (`lifecycle.export_link_days`; none for a final export), the audit entry `organization_export.built`, and `organization_export_ready` to the requester, or to every Tenant Admin for an export of the provider or the final export. A failure deletes the stored parts, marks the row `failed` (`organization_export.failed`) and is thrown again for the one retry; a size refusal or a missing Managed Vault fails without a retry. An organisation that is not active or offboarding fails the export.
- `OrgExportBuilder` writes, in this order: `README.txt` (English and Indonesian), `data/tenant/<table>.csv` and `.jsonl` for every table of the schema (`tenant-data.ts`; every row, deleted ones included, every column minus the excluded ones, read through a server-side cursor, times in ISO 8601 UTC), the twelve files of `data/platform/` (`platform-data.ts`), `audit/audit_trail.jsonl` and `.csv` with `seq`, `prev_hash`, `row_hash`, `redacted_at`, every hashed column and the canonical text of each row (`platform.audit_canonical_text`), `audit/anchors.jsonl` (the format of `GET /admin/audit-trail/anchors`), `audit/audit-anchor.pub` and `audit/anchor-keys.json` (the installation's own public anchor key, which is the public half of `audit.anchor_key_file` and signs the anchors, then the compiled keys; a key the build does not know is listed with a null PEM and `audit-anchor.pub` stays empty, so the export never names a key that did not sign), `files/external-files.csv`, the decrypted content of every Managed Vault file as `files/<repository slug>/<object id>/<file name>`, and the manifest as the last entry of the last part. Disposed content, content removed by the expiry, and content the vault no longer has are listed in the manifest (`filesWithoutContent`) without bytes. The parts and manifests of organisation exports, this one included, are left out of the data and the files.
- `archive/tar-format.ts` extends `tarHeader` of `evidence-packs/tar-stream.ts` with pax extended headers (full UTF-8 path, sizes above 8 GiB); `archive/archive-writer.ts` writes gzipped parts of at most `lifecycle.export_part_bytes` one at a time and cuts an entry larger than a part into pieces; `archive/tar-reader.ts` reads them back for `export verify` and the tests.
- `exclusions.ts`: every tenant table (exported with its excluded columns, or to the audit folder), every platform table with `organization_id` or a foreign key to one (exported with its exported and excluded columns, or excluded), the account tables, and the general items, each with a reason; `exclusionList()` is the manifest's list.
- `platform-data.ts`: a person in `erased_person` appears under the pseudonym only (members), without linked sign-in or sessions, and the platform audit extract replaces every string that is, as a whole, their email (in any case), handle or display name with the pseudonym (text that only contains a short name stays as stored); the network address and browser of platform audit entries whose actor is a platform operator, the system or an erased person are empty.
- `manifest.ts`: format `mscd-organization-export` version 1 (organisation, creation time, requester and approver handles, MSCD version and revision, tenant migrations with their source, framework pack versions per system, parts, entries with part, size, SHA-256, rows and pieces, files without content, audit chain facts, exclusions, counts). `tenantMigrations` is read from the schema's `kysely_migration` table when the role may read it (`tenantMigrationsSource: "schema"`); the application role may not (platform 0001 keeps the `kysely_` tables private to the migrator), so in a running installation it is the build's list, which `mscd-server migrate` applies to every organisation schema before the API and the worker start, marked `"build"`.
- `build-lock.ts`: one build at a time in the installation (D.4), whatever the number of worker processes: the build holds a session advisory lock (class 7004) on one pinned connection, released with the connection if the process dies. The job takes it; a job that finds it held changes nothing, sends itself again 120 seconds later (singleton key `oe:<exportId>:wait:<time>`, because the running job still holds `oe:<exportId>`) and ends with outcome `busy`. `export organization` takes the same lock and refuses while another build runs.

### Expiry (`expiry.ts`, `apps/server/src/modules/jobs/{tenant-cleanup.ts,cleanup-expired.job.ts}`)

- `TenantCleanupStep` takes an optional `statuses` list (default `active`, so the existing steps behave as before), and `cleanup.expired` runs each step for the organisations whose status it lists.
- The org-export module registers `exportsExpired` for every status but `deleted`: a ready export past `expires_at` (a final export once the organisation is `archived`) becomes `expired` and its parts and manifest are deleted with `gateway.objects.delete`; a final export of an organisation that went back to `active` or `suspended` gets an expiry `export_link_days` ahead; a request without a decision for 7 days expires; an export left `queued` or `building` for 24 hours (`STALE_BUILD_HOURS`, by `updated_at`) while no build runs becomes `failed` with a reason (`organization_export.failed`) and the parts it stored are deleted, so a new request can go through; and the vault object of every expired 7-day `export_file` is deleted. A deleted object's `storage_object` row gets `deleted_at`. "No build runs" means the step can take the installation's build lock for its transaction (`pg_try_advisory_xact_lock` on the key of `build-lock.ts`); while it holds it no build can claim or complete a row, and a build only completes a row that is still `building`.

### Command line (`apps/server/src/cli/export.ts`)

- `export organization --out <dir> [--org <slug>] [--anchor-key <file>]`: single tenancy only (multi tenancy refuses and names the web app); the folder must be empty or new; the builder writes the parts there (spools in a hidden working folder inside it, removed at the end) and the full manifest next to them; audited `organization_export.built` with `requestedVia: cli`. The `cli` service of Compose has no private anchor key, so `--anchor-key` gives the build the installation's public key (`secrets/audit-anchor.pub`; a private key is refused) for `audit/audit-anchor.pub` and `audit/anchor-keys.json`; without it the command says which anchor key ids the export lists without a key.
- `export verify --dir <dir> [--anchor-key <file>]...` (`org-export/verify.ts`): the manifest (the downloaded one, else the copy in the last part), every part's size and SHA-256, every entry and piece, every audit hash recomputed from its canonical text with a check that the canonical text holds the row's own values, attested redactions in the format of M3-03's verifier (`{redactionId, rows: [{seq, rowHash, redactedHash}]}` of `tenant.audit_redacted`, `user.audit_redacted`, `privacy.audit_redacted`), and every anchor with the anchor keys compiled into the build (`compiledAnchorKeys`, the default of `verifyExportFolder`) plus the public keys the operator names with `--anchor-key` (repeatable; an on-premise installation signs with the key pair `install.sh` made for it; a private key file is refused), never a key of the export: each anchor must have a valid signature by the trusted key of its `keyId` (with the build's PEM, never the PEM of the export, which is not signed), belong to the export's chain and name the row hash of its `seq`; every key of `audit/anchor-keys.json` must carry its real key id (`anchorKeyId`, SHA-256 of the SPKI), the key of `audit/audit-anchor.pub` must be trusted, and the number of anchors must match the manifest. An anchor without a valid signature by a trusted key is a problem (`anchorKeysTrusted: false`). Any problem prints `FAILED` with the list and exits 1, with a hint to pass `secrets/audit-anchor.pub` when an anchor's key is not trusted; `OK` names the key ids that signed and where each came from (built into the release or the `--anchor-key` file). A redacted row counts as proven (`redactedRows`) only when a valid anchor covers the attestation entry that explains its content (D.5.4); the others are counted apart (`redactedUnanchored`, `redactedUnanchoredSeqs`) and end the proven coverage before the first of them (follow-up round 3). The report gives `newestAnchoredSeq` (the newest row a valid anchor signs), `lastAnchoredSeq` (the newest row the anchors prove, with every row before it) and `rowsAfterLastAnchor`, and the command prints them ("No anchor: no signature proves any of the N audit row(s)" when there is none) with the advice to compare the newest anchor with the anchors kept outside MSCD: the manifest is not signed, so an export whose newest anchors were removed still passes, and only the anchors outside MSCD show it.

## Deviations

1. Platform scope: `withPlatform` is limited by lint to other folders (F.10), so the module uses `inPlatformScope` of `identity/platform-scope.ts` inside the organisation's tenant transaction, for the status changes of `platform.organization_export` (trigger of platform 0007) and for the read of `platform.session` (filtered by the organisation id). The command line uses `withPlatform` once to find the only organisation.
2. The manifest exists in two copies: the full one (`copy: "download"`, the manifest object and its SHA-256 in the export row) and the copy in the last part (`copy: "last-part"`), where the part that holds it has no size and no SHA-256, because a file cannot hold its own hash. The detail route and `X-MSCD-SHA256` give them.
3. An entry larger than a part is cut into pieces (`<path>.001`, `.002`, ...), each filling a part of its own; the manifest lists them under the entry and the README says how to join them. The plan does not say what happens to such an entry.
4. A manifest download is audited, counted and announced like a part download (H.2 "each download").
5. `export organization` takes `--org <slug>` (default: the only organisation), because a single-tenancy installation may hold more than one organisation (the demo holds four). It writes no `platform.organization_export` row; its audit entry and manifest name `requestedVia: cli`.
6. Message keys beyond J.6 on the existing slugs: `export_not_requested` and `export_request_expired` (409 on a decision), `export_not_ready` (409 on a download of an export that is not ready or has expired), `export_not_available` (409 on an operator export of an organisation that is not active or offboarding).
7. A Managed Vault file whose content the gateway no longer has (404) is listed in `filesWithoutContent` with reason `missing`, and the build goes on, so one lost file never blocks the final export of an organisation. Any other gateway error fails the build.
8. The expiry marks a deleted object's `storage_object` row with `deleted_at` (no code soft-deleted storage objects before); the export lists such objects as removed content.
9. `platform.invitation` is classified as excluded (the token hash is a secret; who invited whom and when is in `members`), and `platform.audit_anchor` as exported (`audit/anchors`). Both choices are written with their reasons in `exclusions.ts`.
10. One build per installation at a time (`build-lock.ts`, a session advisory lock), so the Helm worker's autoscaling and Compose replicas keep the D.4 rule. A busy job sends itself again under a singleton key of its own (`oe:<exportId>:wait:<time>`); a second job of the same export that runs later finds the export `ready` and skips.
11. `apps/server/test/int/m2-pending/M3-02.json` is deleted in the working tree only; the deletion is not staged (`git status` shows ` D`, `git diff --cached` is empty). The orchestrator includes it in the wave commit with `git rm apps/server/test/int/m2-pending/M3-02.json`. This package touched no other part of the index.
12. The tables and platform files are read one after another, each in its own tenant transaction, so an export of an organisation that keeps working is not one snapshot: a row written during the build may be in one file and its referenced row missing from another. A single REPEATABLE READ snapshot needs the isolation level set before the first statement of the transaction, and `TenantScope.withTenant` (owned by M3-03 in wave 2) runs its `set_config` first; the README of the export (both languages) and section 19.3 of the guide state it and advise pausing work for an export that matches one moment.

13. `export verify` takes `--anchor-key <file>` (repeatable) and `export organization` takes `--anchor-key <file>`: D.4 names neither, but an on-premise installation signs its anchors with a key pair of its own (`ensure_anchor_key` of `install.sh`), which no release compiles in, and the `cli` service has no private anchor key. Verify still trusts no key that only the export names.
14. The expiry fails an export left `queued` or `building` for 24 hours while no build runs (round 2); D.4 and J.8 name no such move. It uses the existing `failed` status and audit action. Round 3 adds the moves the code makes to `organization_export.status` in `packages/shared/src/machines.ts` (`failed` to `building`, `queued` to `failed`, and the stale expiry in the note of `building` to `failed`); the J.8 row of `MILESTONE-3.md` still lists the older set (outside this package's paths: needsFromOrchestrator).
15. The worker's temporary folder in Compose is the named volume `worker-tmp`, or a host folder named in `MSCD_WORKER_TMP` (round 3). The variable is documented in the comment of `docker-compose.yml` and in section 19.1; `.env.example` does not list it (outside this package's paths).

## Follow-ups

- **Deploy:** done in follow-up round 3 (the worker's scratch volume `worker-tmp`, the Helm `emptyDir`, the folder in the image; see that section).
- **Snapshot (a later package, `modules/tenancy/**`):** an option of `withTenant` for a REPEATABLE READ, read-only transaction (`db.transaction().setIsolationLevel('repeatable read')` before the `set_config`) would let the builder read every table and platform file in one snapshot (deviation 12).
- **Tenant migrations in the manifest (a later migration):** a grant of SELECT on each tenant schema's `kysely_migration` to `mscd_app` (in `platform.apply_tenant_grants` of a new platform migration) would let the manifest name the migrations actually applied (`tenantMigrationsSource: "schema"`); the code already reads them when the grant exists.
- **M3-03:** queue the final export at the move to `offboarding` by inserting `platform.organization_export` (status `queued`, `requested_via 'offboarding'`, `is_final true`, `requested_by null`) and sending `org-export.build` with `{orgId, payload: {exportId}}`, singleton key `oe:<exportId>`, retry limit 1. The unique index allows one `queued` or `building` row per organisation, so check for one first (when an export of a Tenant Admin is building, mark it final or queue the final one after it). The expiry removes the content of the final export when the organisation is `archived`; the row stays for the certificate.
- **M3-09:** the screen reads the routes above; the manifest downloads as `<base>-manifest.json`, parts carry `X-MSCD-SHA256`, refusals carry the message keys of J.6 and deviation 6 with messages in both languages.
- **M3-11:** `KIND_SCREENS` for `organization_export_requested` and `organization_export_ready` (screen `org-export`).
- **M3-04 (wave 3, owner of the two jobs files then):** `TenantCleanupStep.statuses` is there for the technical personal data of D.6.7 if it must run beyond active organisations.
- The verifier of `export verify` follows the attestation format of M3-03's `chain-verifier.ts` as it is in wave 2; a change of that format must change `verify.ts` too.
- Anchor key rotation: `export verify` trusts the anchor keys compiled into the build that runs it and the keys named with `--anchor-key`, so a release that drops an anchor key can verify exports whose anchors that key signed only when the operator names the old public key. Keep retired anchor keys in `TRUSTED_KEYS` (D-16) for SaaS, and tell on-premise operators to keep every `secrets/audit-anchor.pub` they used (section 19.3 says so).
- **Core, next to the M2-S05 K.9 item (outside M3-02):** the in-app anchor checks (`AuditChainVerifier`, `audit.verify-chain`, the audit trail screen) still trust the compiled keys only (`AUDIT_ANCHOR_KEYS`), so on-premise they report "not among the anchor keys compiled into this build" for the installation's own key. `export verify` no longer depends on that (it takes `--anchor-key`), and the export now carries the installation's real public key. The fix M2-S05 proposed (trust the installation's own `audit-anchor.pub` read at start, with an audit entry when first trusted, or compile the customer's key into the customer's release) belongs to the owner of `modules/audit/**`.
- **Unsigned manifest (design note):** the manifest and parts are not signed, so an export whose newest anchors were removed and whose newer rows were rewritten still passes `export verify`; the command now says up to which row the anchors prove the trail and tells the operator to compare with the anchors kept outside MSCD. Signing the manifest with the anchor key at build time (worker only) would close this for web exports; it needs a decision on the format and on command line exports, which have no private key.
