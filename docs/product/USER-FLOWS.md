# MSCD user flows

Checked by an ISO lead-auditor review, a code accuracy check and a cross-flow completeness check. A human lead auditor should still confirm it before customers see it. Milestone 2 (docs/platform/MILESTONE-2.md) builds the screens for the steps marked Partial or Not built.

Four user flows that show who does what in MSCD, in which screen, and why an auditor cares. Status per step: **Available** (works in the app), **Partial** (command line, API or with limits), **Not built**.

## The journey

1. **Onboard the customer.** Set up the organisation, sign-in, management systems and storage.
2. **Assign people.** Give each person a role and make them owner of their work.
3. **Prepare documents.** Write and approve the documents, collect the records that prove them.
4. **Work the clauses.** Go clause by clause and control by control until each one is verified.
5. **Certification audit.** Stage 1 checks the documents, stage 2 checks that you follow them.
6. **Stay certified.** Surveillance audits every year, recertification every three years.

## Customer onboarding

Takes a signed customer from an empty server, or a new SaaS organisation, to an organisation in MSCD. At the end, each licensed management system exists with its scope, edition, file storage and audit dates, and the core team can sign in with the right roles. This matters for certification because the scope, the role assignments and the access controls set up here are among the first things a certification auditor checks.

**When:** Use this flow when a customer has signed the contract and needs a working MSCD organisation. It covers everything up to the day the core team starts implementation work on the first management system. For every later change of people and roles, and for the access reviews, use user-and-role-assignment. Next flows, in order: user-and-role-assignment, document-preparation-and-prerequisites, working-through-the-clauses.

| Management System Sponsor (Programme Owner) | Programme Owner | A senior person who oversees the effort. Can verify work, approve documents, reassign items and record management reviews. Cannot create or change other records. Sees everyone's pending work. |
| Anti-bribery compliance function (ISO 37001 only) | No separate role. Recorded, for example, as the primary Management System Manager of the anti-bribery system | Oversees the anti-bribery system. | Control owner or process owner | Domain Owner | Owns controls, documents and evidence in one area and works mainly from My Tasks. |
| Risk owner | Risk Owner | Owns risks, approves the risk treatment plan for them and accepts the residual risk (the risk left after treatment) on their own risks (ISO 27001 clause 6.1.3 f). |

### Before you start

- A signed contract and a licence order with CyberLentera that lists the frameworks, the number of users (active members without guests), the number of management systems, the storage adapters (m365, google_drive) and the features needed (for example auditor_guest_access, link_health_monitor, edition_transition). If setup must start before the bound licence arrives, ask for an unbound trial licence (up to 90 days).
- A server: 64-bit Linux on x86-64 with Docker Engine 24 or later, the Docker Compose plugin 2.20 or later, openssl, sha256sum, tar, curl and sudo access. For production up to 150 users: 8 vCPU, 32 GB RAM, 500 GB SSD. For Kubernetes: version 1.29 or later, Helm 3, an internal registry, an ingress controller, PostgreSQL 15 or later and an S3-compatible store.
- A host name in DNS and a TLS certificate and key from the company certificate authority. A self-signed certificate works for a trial, but every browser shows a warning.
- Email relay details for invitations and notifications: SMTP host, port, account, password file and sender address. They are passed to install.sh as options, because the installer does not ask for them.
- Firewall openings from the server to Microsoft and Google over HTTPS when Entra ID, SharePoint Online or Google Drive will be used.
- The final organisation short name (slug: a lower-case letter followed by 2 to 40 lower-case letters, digits or underscores) and the display name. The slug can never change.
- Work emails of two people who will be Tenant Admins, and of a third if admins will grant business roles to each other.
- For each management system, a list of the internal and external issues and the interested parties with their requirements (clauses 4.1 and 4.2).
- A draft scope statement for each management system (up to 4000 characters), built on those issues and requirements and agreed by top management. It is made final and signed before Stage 1.
- The chosen certification body, whose accreditation covers the standard and the industry sector, the lead auditor name if known, the audit days from its quotation, and target dates for the Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits.
- A role list per management system: primary Management System Manager, a Management System Sponsor (Programme Owner) if a senior person will oversee the effort, Domain Owners, Risk Owners, Internal Auditor and Top Management viewers. For ISO 37001, also the anti-bribery compliance function and any Compliance Investigator.
- HR checks and signed terms are done for each person on the role list, and top management has a competence record for each role holder (see user-and-role-assignment).
- For single sign-on: an Entra ID (or other OIDC or SAML) administrator who can register apps, and the company email domain if accounts should be created automatically.
- For customer file storage: a SharePoint site with an admin who can grant the Sites.Selected permission, or a Google Shared Drive with a service account or a Google account that gives delegated consent. Inside it, one root folder per management system, each with a Documents folder and Evidence/<year>/Q<quarter> folders. A personal My Drive is allowed by default, but it ties company records to one person, so a Shared Drive is the safer choice.
- A safe place, such as the company password safe, for the copy of the secrets folder and the recovery codes.

### Decisions

- **Will MSCD run on the customer's own server with Docker Compose?** Yes: Run install.sh with --admin-email and the SMTP options. It creates the organisation and prints the first admin link. No: On Kubernetes, install with Helm and create the organisation and first admin with kubectl exec and node apps/server/dist/main.js. On SaaS, the Platform Operator creates the organisation with tenant create.
- **Does the organisation work outside the Asia/Jakarta time zone or across several sites?** Yes: The Compose installer cannot set either value and nothing changes them later. Raise it with CyberLentera before installing. On Kubernetes or SaaS, pass --time-zone and --site-count to tenant create. No: Keep the installer defaults.
- **Has the bound licence arrived before setup starts?** Yes: Install it with PUT /admin/license and check that the state is valid. No: Ask CyberLentera for an unbound trial licence of up to 90 days, install it, and replace it with the bound licence before it ends.
- **Will there be two Tenant Admins from the start?** Yes: Each admin asks for their own business role and the other one approves it. A business role that one admin gives the other waits for a third Tenant Admin, so invite a third admin if that is needed. No: Give the primary Management System Manager role to a person who holds no admin role, because that grant is active at once. A lone Tenant Admin cannot activate a business role for themselves.
- **Does the customer use Microsoft Entra ID or another OIDC or SAML identity provider?** Yes: Connect and test it, invite the people, then set the local sign-in policy to admins_only. No: Keep local sign-in allowed. Every person uses a password and an authenticator app. Direct LDAP sign-in is not available.
- **Should accounts be created automatically at the first SSO sign-in?** Yes: Use an OIDC provider whose tokens mark emails as verified (Entra ID usually does not), have the operator verify the email domain, then set jitProvisioning true and allowedEmailDomains on the provider. Each new account takes a licence seat. Roles are still granted by hand. No: Invite every person with POST /admin/users.
- **Will documents and evidence stay in SharePoint Online or Google Drive?** Yes: Check that the licence includes m365 or google_drive. Prefer a Google Shared Drive over a personal My Drive. Create the systems first, then connect the repository with one root per system, each with a Documents folder and Evidence/<year>/Q<quarter> folders, and point each system at it. No: Use the Managed Vault, which is ready right after installation.
- **Is an anti-bribery management system (ISO 37001) part of the order?** Yes: Use the ISO37001-2025 beta pack and confirm with the certification body which edition they will audit against. Existing 2016 certificates stay valid until the transition ends. Name the anti-bribery compliance function and add a Compliance Investigator if concerns will be investigated in MSCD. No: No action needed.
- **Will the quality management system be certified to ISO 9001:2026?** Yes: Choose pack ISO9001-2026 with edition 2026. Its content still follows the 2015 edition for now. No: First ask the certification body whether it still offers first-time certification to the 2015 edition. If it does, choose ISO9001-2015 and plan the move to the new edition before the transition end date set by IAF. Check the current IAF notice with the certification body.
- **Has the risk assessment been done for the ISO 27001 or ISO 37001 system?** Yes: Load the control or requirement list with the applicability decisions from the risk treatment, then link the risks in working-through-the-clauses so the entries stop showing Blocked. No: Load the list after the risk assessment in working-through-the-clauses, or load it now as a draft and expect every applicable entry to show Blocked until a risk is linked.
- **Are the Stage 1 and Stage 2 dates agreed with the certification body?** Yes: The Management System Manager records both audit events right after the roles are granted, with the audit days from the body's quotation. No: Audit readiness shows 'No audit event scheduled' until the dates are recorded.

- A running MSCD installation from a checked bundle, with TLS, a working email relay and a safe copy of the secrets folder kept apart from the database backups.
- An organisation with its final slug, its Managed Vault and the audit trail entry tenant.created.
- Two Tenant Admins (three when admins will grant business roles to each other), each with a password, an authenticator app and saved recovery codes.
- A valid licence bound to the installation id on-premise or to the organisation id on SaaS, or a trial licence until the bound one arrives.
- The chosen sign-in method: a tested identity provider and a local sign-in policy, or local sign-in with two-step codes for everyone.
- A list of issues and interested parties per system, ready to enter in the context issue and interested party registers, and a scope built on them.
- One management system per licensed standard with its edition, scope statement, certification body and default repository, in status implementing.

### Common mistakes

- Picking the organisation slug casually. It can never change, and a second install with another slug is refused.
- Installing with the defaults for an organisation outside the Jakarta time zone. Nightly checks, reminders and the 07:00 digest then run on Jakarta time.
- Running the installer without the SMTP options. It never asks for them, mail goes to a host that does not exist, and nobody receives an invitation.
- Forgetting to copy the secrets folder. Without it the database and the encrypted files cannot be opened after a failure.
- Expecting the Tenant Admin to see the business screens. The admin role carries no business permission, so the admin sees only the 'no role' notice until a business role is active.
- Starting with a single Tenant Admin who is also meant to be the Management System Manager. The self-grant stays pending until a second Tenant Admin approves it.
- Expecting two Tenant Admins to grant business roles to each other. The approver may be neither the requester nor the receiver, so such a grant waits for a third Tenant Admin. With two admins, each asks for their own role and the other approves it.
- Giving the Programme Owner role to the person who will run the project day to day. That role is meant for a senior sponsor and cannot create or change other records, so the daily lead needs the Management System Manager role.
- Trying to invite the team before the licence is installed. Every invitation after the first admin fails with LIC-USERS.
- Inviting people or granting roles before the HR checks, the signed terms and the competence record are done. Auditors compare those dates with the dates of the grants.
- Re-sending a link to a Tenant Admin with admin create-user and leaving out --tenant-admin, or using it for a Storage Admin. The command quietly removes their admin role.
- Creating an anti-bribery system on ISO 37001:2016. That edition is withdrawn in MSCD and the request is refused.
- Choosing ISO 9001:2015 for a first certification without asking the certification body whether it still certifies new customers to that edition.
- Connecting a repository before the systems exist, or keying its roots by the upper-case code. The server expects the slug of an existing system.
- Building the storage folders without a Documents folder and Evidence/<year>/Q<quarter> folders, or putting files in deeper subfolders. The file picker then cannot find the files.
- Assuming SSO matches people by email. Every person still needs an invitation or a pre-registered identity.
- Turning on automatic accounts with Entra ID. Its tokens usually carry no verified email, so no account is created.
- Setting local sign-in to disabled before SSO has been tested with a second account, which can lock everyone out.
- Writing the scope before listing the issues and the interested parties (clauses 4.1 and 4.2).
- Listing Annex A controls that do not apply as scope exclusions. They belong in the Statement of Applicability, and clauses 4 to 10 can never be left out.
- Treating the scope text in MSCD as the controlled scope document. It has no version and no approval, so the signed scope must also live in the Document register.
- Loading all Annex A controls as applicable before the risk assessment. Each one shows Blocked until a risk is linked, so the checkpoint list fills with 93 blockers.
- Treating the loaded control list as the final Statement of Applicability before the risk assessment and the risk treatment are done.
- Giving the Management System Manager and the Internal Auditor role of one system to the same person. The Management System Manager would then audit their own work, and the audit would lose its objectivity. A small organisation can use a colleague from another unit or an outside auditor.
- Forgetting the anti-bribery compliance function when setting up an ISO 37001 system.
- Guessing the audit days. The certification body sets them in its quotation and audit plan.
- Adding a preparation task by hand before the preparation window opens. The daily job then adds none of the pack's tasks to that audit.
- Leaving the pack's preparation tasks unchanged. Their titles come from the demo organisations and name records the customer does not have.
- Counting the three-year certificate validity, or the surveillance and recertification dates, from the start of the project. They follow from the date the certification body decides to grant the certificate.
- Letting invitation links expire. They are valid for 72 hours only.
- Comparing a fresh customer with the demo. A new system starts with no controls, no cycle checklist and no traceability chains.

## User and role assignment

This flow gives each person the access and the responsibility their job needs in each management system, and takes it away when they no longer need it. Certification auditors check that top management has assigned and communicated the roles (clause 5.3); that each risk has a named risk owner who approved the treatment plan and accepted the remaining risk (ISO 27001 clauses 6.1.2 c) and 6.1.3 f)); that planned actions for risks, objectives and corrective actions have an owner and a target date (ISO 27001 clauses 6.1.3, 6.2 and 10.2); that the people in these roles are competent, with records to show it (clause 7.2); and that access rights are given, reviewed and removed in a controlled way, with separate people for conflicting tasks where the risk calls for it (ISO 27001 Annex A 5.3, 5.15, 5.18 and 8.2).

**When:** For the first setup of an organisation or a management system, follow customer-onboarding. Use this flow for every later change and for the access reviews: when someone joins, changes job or leaves, when a piece of work needs a new owner or a new date, and at each scheduled access review. Also use it before each certification audit: before stage 1 (the review of documents and readiness), again before stage 2 (the main audit of how the system works), and before each yearly surveillance audit, because the external auditor needs new access each time. The flows run in this order: customer-onboarding, user-and-role-assignment, document-preparation-and-prerequisites, working-through-the-clauses.

### Before you start

- customer-onboarding is complete: MSCD is installed, the organisation exists, and the first Tenant Admin has accepted the invitation, set a password and added an authenticator app.
- Each management system to be staffed exists. It was created in customer-onboarding with POST /admin/management-systems, which is API only.
- A licence is installed with enough user seats (otherwise inviting fails with LIC-USERS). Guest auditor access needs the licence feature auditor_guest_access.
- Outgoing email (SMTP) is set with the installer options, because invitation links, reminders and guest links arrive by email.
- Top management has decided, in writing, who holds which responsibility in each system. MSCD has no place to record this decision.
- HR has completed background checks and the person has signed terms that include their information security duties before an invitation is sent (ISO 27001 Annex A 6.1 and 6.2; for ISO 37001 also the hiring checks of clause 7.2.2).
- The Tenant Admin can use the browser console helper mscd(method, path, body) from docs/INSTALL-ONPREM.md section 7, since most people administration has no screen.
- For single sign-on users, the identity provider (for example Microsoft Entra ID) is connected.

### Decisions

- **Have HR checks and signed terms been completed for the person?** Yes: Send the invitation. No: Wait. An auditor sampling a new joiner compares the dates of screening, signed terms and first access.
- **Has the person accepted the invitation yet?** Yes: Grant business roles now, using the handle that GET /admin/users shows after acceptance. No: Wait. A business role can only be given to an active member, so the grant is refused with '<handle> is not a member of this organisation' (NOT-A-MEMBER).
- **Does the person already have an active MSCD account in another organisation?** Yes: Inviting adds a membership but sends no email. Tell the person yourself. They accept with POST /me/memberships/{org}/accept after signing in. The Tenant Admin cannot change their name, account status or second factor. No: The person gets an email link valid for 72 hours and joins on the 'Join MSCD' page.
- **Will the person sign in with company single sign-on?** Yes: Let the person pick the provider button on 'Join MSCD' when they open the invitation. Otherwise register their provider identity with POST /admin/users/{id}/identities. No: They use a password and an authenticator app.
- **Is the person competent for the role, with a record to show it?** Yes: Grant the role and keep the record with the role decision. No: Plan training or choose another person before the grant, and keep proof of the training.
- **Is the role grant for yourself, or for someone who is Tenant Admin or Storage Admin?** Yes: The grant waits for a Tenant Admin who is neither you nor the person receiving it. A grant to yourself can be approved by the other Tenant Admin. A grant to the other Tenant Admin needs a third one, so let each admin ask for their own role. No: The grant is active at once.
- **Will this person run the management system day to day?** Yes: Grant Management System Manager. No: For a senior person who only oversees the effort, grant Programme Owner (Management System Sponsor).
- **Is this person the main manager of the system?** Yes: Grant Management System Manager with isPrimary true. If another person holds the primary grant, revoke that first. No: Grant Management System Manager without the primary mark.
- **Does the internal auditor own any of the work they will audit?** Yes: Choose another internal auditor for that area. The platform will not warn you. No: Grant Internal Auditor.
- **Is the item a document step, a preparation task, a finding or a corrective action?** Yes: Use the API: POST /ms/{ms}/records/document/{id}/assignment for a document step, POST /ms/{ms}/records/audit_prep_task/{id}/assignment for a preparation task (Management System Manager or Programme Owner), and PATCH for findings and corrective actions (Management System Manager only). No: Use 'Reassign' or 'Change due date' on the screen.
- **Does the external auditor already have a regular MSCD account?** Yes: The Tenant Admin adds a membership and an External Auditor role with an end date through the API. No: Use 'Auditor access' on Audit readiness to create a guest login.
- **Does the audit stage need more or fewer than 14 days of access?** Yes: Create the grant through the API with expiresInDays from 1 to 30. No: Use the 'Auditor access' button. Either way, create a new grant for each audit stage.
- **Does the auditor ask for records that are not in the ready pack?** Yes: Submit the evidence and build a new pack the same day. Show document files and records kept outside MSCD another way. No: The auditor works from the current pack.
- **Does the leaver still hold open items?** Yes: Reassign every item first, then revoke roles and disable the membership. No: Revoke roles and disable the membership.
- **Does the leaver belong to another MSCD organisation?** Yes: Disable only the membership in this organisation. No: Disable the membership and the account.

### Common mistakes

- Making someone Tenant Admin and expecting them to see the screens. Admin roles carry no business permission, so the person sees only the 'You hold no role' notice until they get a business role.
- Giving an admin Domain Owner, Internal Auditor or Top Management and expecting Framework library and Storage repositories. Those screens show only for Management System Manager or Programme Owner.
- Running the organisation with one Tenant Admin, or expecting two to be enough for every grant. The approver can be neither the requester nor the person receiving the role, so with two Tenant Admins a grant from one admin to the other waits for a third. Let each admin ask for their own business role and the other approve it.
- Giving Programme Owner to the project lead who runs the work day to day. That role is for a senior sponsor and cannot create or change records, so the project lead needs Management System Manager.
- Giving access before HR checks and signed terms are done. An auditor sampling a new joiner checks the dates.
- Assigning a role without a competence record, above all for internal auditors.
- Granting a business role before the person has accepted the invitation, or with the handle the member list shows before acceptance. Only active members can receive a role, and the real handle can differ.
- Pasting the system slug (for example 'isms') into the role routes. They need the system UUID from GET /admin/management-systems.
- Expecting a person who already uses MSCD elsewhere to get an invitation email. Only new accounts and accounts never activated get one.
- Disabling a leaver before handing over their work. Nothing moves their items, reminders to them are refused, and their role grants stay in place, so re-enabling brings every role back.
- Reassigning an item and assuming the new owner knows. Only preparation tasks send a notice, and it arrives in the next morning's digest, so send a reminder right after.
- Choosing a new holder who has no role in that system. The server accepts it, but My Tasks lists items only from systems where the person holds a role, so the item can stay out of their sight.
- Looking for 'Reassign' on a document. No screen has it, including the Document rows on Chain stages. Use the API assignment route, which changes who must do the next step while the document owner stays the same.
- Naming an approver on a document and expecting only that person to approve. The field is a label: any Management System Manager or Programme Owner other than the submitter can approve.
- Expecting the risk owner to approve the treatment in MSCD. Only the Management System Manager can, so keep the risk owner's signed approval of the plan as a separate record.
- Setting every planned risk treatment action before the audit just to clear the After audit flag. Auditors accept later dates in an approved plan that is moving forward, and false dates cause a worse finding.
- Reading the demo role menu as the roles of a live install. The demo offers four sample people; in a live install the top bar and the role menu show the label of the role each person holds, such as External Auditor, Programme Owner or Risk Owner.
- Letting the internal auditor also own controls or risks in the area they audit. Auditors expect internal auditors to be independent of the work they check.
- Planning one person per system. The person who completes an item cannot verify it, so each system needs a second person with verify rights.
- Giving the external auditor access before a ready evidence pack exists. The guest can open files only inside ready packs.
- Treating the evidence pack as the whole audit. The auditor chooses samples during the audit, so be ready to add records and build a new pack the same day.
- Expecting the auditor to see owners in MSCD. The guest sees no names, so add a roles and owners list to the pack.
- Forgetting that guest access from the button always ends after 14 days and cannot be extended. Create a new grant for stage 1, for stage 2 and for each surveillance audit.
- Sending a second reminder about the same item to the same person within 72 hours. It is refused.
- Keeping the access review under leavers only. It covers every user and needs its own schedule and record.

## Document preparation and prerequisites

Prepare the documented information an ISO audit expects: the documents that say how the organisation works and the records that prove it worked. Then clear the prerequisites that must be met before a phase is signed off or the certification audit can pass: checkpoint items, phase sign-offs, a Stage 1 readiness check and periodic items such as the internal audit and the management review. Auditors judge a management system mostly by reading these documents and sampling these records, so a gap here usually becomes an audit finding.

**When:** Use this flow when a new management system starts implementation, when a Phase board activity names a required output document, when a document reaches its review date, when evidence turns Aging or Expired, before you book the Stage 1 audit, and during the 60 days before a certification audit. In a Stage 1 audit the certification auditor reads your documents and checks whether you are ready for the main audit. In a Stage 2 audit the auditor checks that the system works in practice by sampling records and talking to staff. A surveillance audit is the shorter check the certification body makes in each year between certifications.

### Who is involved

| Role | Shown in MSCD as | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Top management | Top Management | Sets the direction of the management system, endorses the top-level policy and objectives, confirms that the system is ready before the Stage 1 audit, and takes part in the management review. In MSCD this role can read and export, and cannot approve documents. |
| Management system manager (the person given responsibility and authority for the ISMS) | Management System Manager (shown with a per-system title such as ISMS Manager) | Runs the document register: creates entries, sets owners, approvers and review dates, links documents to controls, verifies completed work and signs off phases. Can approve documents that someone else submitted. In MSCD this is also the only role that can approve a risk treatment. |
| Management System Sponsor (Programme Owner) | Programme Owner | A senior person who oversees the effort. Can verify work, approve documents, reassign items and record management reviews. Cannot create or change other records such as documents, risks, controls or evidence. Useful as the second person who approves what the Management System Manager wrote. |
| Document owner (author) | Owner column in the Document register, usually a Domain Owner | Writes and updates the document, submits each new version for review, and starts the review when the review date comes. |
| Document approver (authorised approver) | Approver field on the document | Checks that a version is correct and fit for use, then approves it or returns it with a note. Must hold Management System Manager or Programme Owner (Management System Sponsor) and must be a different person from the one who submitted the version. |
| Process or control owner (record owner) | Owner on controls and evidence, usually a Domain Owner | Runs the control day to day and submits the records that prove it ran, on time for its operating frequency. Can also create and change their own evidence records. |
| Risk owner | Domain Owner (business role risk_owner) | Takes part in assessing their risks, updates the progress of treatments for their own risks, approves the treatment plan for their risks and accepts the remaining risk. ### Before you start

- A management system exists for the standard and edition, for example ISO/IEC 27001:2022, and is in status implementing. Today this is set up through the API (POST /admin/management-systems). For an anti-bribery system use the ISO37001-2025 pack: MSCD marks the 2016 edition as withdrawn and refuses it.
- Each person in this flow holds a business role in that system: a Management System Manager, a Management System Sponsor (Programme Owner) or a second Management System Manager so that the author and the approver can be different people, and the document, evidence and risk owners.
- The context of the organisation (clause 4.1) and the interested parties with their requirements (clause 4.2) are worked out and written down. They can be stored through the API in the context issues and interested parties registers (POST /ms/{ms}/registers/context-issues and POST /ms/{ms}/registers/interested-parties). The legal, regulatory and contract requirements found here also feed the list of such requirements that ISO 27001 control A.5.31 asks for.
- The scope of the management system (clause 4.3) is decided from that context and those requirements, and written in the system's scope statement.
- A file repository is ready. Managed Vault works right after installation. Connecting a SharePoint or Google Drive repository is done through the API today (POST /admin/storage/repositories), because 'Connect repository' on Storage repositories only shows the message 'Repository connection wizard opened'. Give each system a root folder with a Documents folder and Evidence/<year>/Q<quarter> folders. The file picker opens those folders first, falls back to the system root folder when they are empty, and does not open subfolders.
- The team agrees a numbering scheme that uses the exact codes the framework pack expects for activity outputs. For ISO 27001 these are POL-01, PRC-05, SOA-01, PRC-07, PRC-08, PRC-11, PRC-13 and IMS-01 to IMS-04. ISO 22301 adds PLN-B01 and PLN-B02, and ISO 9001 adds QM-01. Document codes are unique across the whole organisation.

### Decisions

- **Does this item say how the organisation works, such as a policy, procedure or plan?** Yes: Treat it as a document: give it a register entry with tier, owner, approver and review date, and send each version through review and approval. No: Treat it as a record: submit it as evidence against the control it proves. It is kept as produced and judged by its age.
- **Is it a register or list that you keep up to date, such as the asset inventory or the risk register?** Yes: Keep it current like a document, with an owner and a review date. When you need proof of its state at a point in time, submit a dated copy or export as a record. No: Use the first decision to treat it as a document or a record.
- **Does a Phase board activity name an output code for this document?** Yes: Create the register entry with that exact code before marking the activity complete (BR-40). If another register document already covers it, the API lets you name that document with outputDocumentCode when you complete the activity. No: Use a code from your own numbering scheme and keep the activity's result as evidence. The API can still record the document as the activity's output with outputDocumentCode.
- **Is the document used by more than one management system?** Yes: Either mark it organisation-wide so every system uses it, or link it to each system that uses it. Expect a confirmation step on each change (BR-22). No: Link it only to the system it serves.
- **Are the risk assessment method (PRC-05) approved and the risk assessment done, with treatments chosen?** Yes: Get each risk owner's signed approval of the plan and acceptance of the planned remaining risk, then record the Statement of Applicability decisions and approve SOA-01. MSCD records the risk owner's acceptance itself later, once each treatment is Implemented. No: Finish those first. Control decisions made before the risk assessment have no basis that an auditor will accept.
- **Is a risk treatment now Implemented?** Yes: Ask the risk owner to confirm acceptance of the remaining risk in MSCD. If the risk owner implemented the treatment, MSCD refuses this (BR-04), so plan the work so that someone else carries it out. No: Keep the treatment in progress and update it. It cannot be closed before the risk owner accepts the remaining risk.
- **For ISO 27001: is the Annex A control applicable in the Statement of Applicability?** Yes: Record why it is included (a risk treatment, or a legal, regulatory or contract requirement) and whether it is implemented. Before it can be verified it needs an implementing document, a linked risk (an MSCD rule, BR-02) and current evidence. No: Write a real reason for leaving it out. Fillers such as 'n/a' give a warning. No document or evidence is needed for it.
- **Are the files kept in SharePoint or Google Drive?** Yes: Use link mode with 'Link selected file', and leave the file where it is afterwards. A moved file still counts but shows in the 'File cannot be verified' list. A deleted file lowers the control status. No: Upload to Managed Vault, which keeps versions and a SHA-256 fingerprint.
- **Has the document passed its review date?** Yes: It is Expired. Press 'Start review', update or confirm the content, and get it approved by someone other than the submitter. No: Plan the review in the Review calendar and act on the alerts at 60, 30, 14 and 7 days.
- **Does the content of an approved document need to change?** Yes: Use 'Upload new version'. The new version goes to In review and needs approval by someone other than the submitter. Meanwhile the document shows In review, and it shows Draft if the version is returned, while the earlier approved file stays in force. No: Keep the current version. At the review date, approve the review with no change.
- **Is the evidence Fresh or Aging, produced in the current period, with an intact or moved file?** Yes: The control can be verified to Implemented. No: Submit or re-link a current file. Until then the control cannot be verified.
- **Is a phase activity stuck because of a checkpoint item?** Yes: Record a blocker that names the checkpoint item and a reason, then fix the checkpoint item. No: Complete the activity and ask a different person to verify its output.
- **Is the next activity shown as Locked on the Phase board?** Yes: Verify every activity of the previous phase and sign off that phase. Meanwhile you can still write and approve the documents the activity needs. No: Start the activity and complete it with its output document.
- **Before booking Stage 1: are the scope, policy, risk method, risk assessment, Statement of Applicability, treatment plan and objectives approved, and are the internal audit and the management review dated?** Yes: Ask top management to confirm the Stage 1 readiness checklist, then book Stage 1. No: Finish or date the missing items first. The Stage 1 auditor reads them to decide whether you are ready for Stage 2.
- **Before booking Stage 2: are the internal audit and the management review done, and are their findings being acted on?** Yes: Book the Stage 2 audit and work through the preparation checklist. No: Finish one internal audit of the whole management system and one management review that uses its results, and leave time to act on the findings before Stage 2.

### What you have at the end

- A document register in which every required document is Approved, with its owner, approver, current version and next review date.
- A stored version trail per document: each version with who submitted it, who approved it and when (not yet viewable on screen).
- Shared organisation-wide procedures (IMS-01 to IMS-04) used by every system that needs them.
- An approved risk assessment method (PRC-05), recorded risk assessment results, a risk treatment plan signed by each risk owner with their acceptance of the planned remaining risk, and an approved Statement of Applicability (SOA-01).
- For each treatment that is Implemented, the risk owner's acceptance of the remaining risk recorded in MSCD.
- Each applicable control linked to its implementing document and to at least one current evidence record.
- Evidence files with pinned versions and fingerprints, each with a freshness state of Fresh or Aging.
- Verified Phase board activities and a verified critical checkpoint phase (Risk & SoA for ISO 27001).
- A Stage 1 readiness checklist confirmed by top management.
- An internal audit programme and its reports, and a management review record that used the audit results, finished before Stage 2.
- A completed audit preparation checklist and an evidence pack with its manifest and list of left-out items.
- Exported Statement of Applicability, including the implementation status of each control, and document register files (XLSX) for the auditor.

### Common mistakes

- Showing the auditor a procedure as proof that a control works. The auditor will also ask for records from the current cycle.
- Deciding which Annex A controls apply before the risk assessment method is approved and the risk assessment is done.
- Creating a risk only to clear a checkpoint item. A control included because of a law or a contract should say so in its justification.
- Expecting to record the risk owner's acceptance of remaining risk in MSCD while the plan is made. MSCD accepts it only once the treatment is Implemented, so keep a signed plan for the planning stage.
- Letting the risk owner carry out their own treatment. MSCD then refuses their acceptance of the remaining risk (BR-04).
- Treating the asset inventory or the risk register as a record that is never updated. Keep the list current and submit dated exports as proof.
- Submitting last month's or last year's records through the screen, which stamps them with today's date and makes them look fresh.
- Planning for the author to approve their own document. MSCD refuses this (BR-16), so name a second approver from the start.
- Using a document code that differs from the Phase board output code, so the activity cannot be marked complete.
- Planning to finish activities of two phases at the same time on the Phase board. An activity that has not started stays Locked until the previous phase is verified.
- Letting review dates pass. A document still in use after its review date is a common audit finding.
- Keeping the scope only in the system's scope text, with no controlled scope document behind it.
- Writing 'n/a' or 'not relevant' as the reason for leaving out an Annex A control.
- Linking evidence produced at go-live. The auditor wants proof that the control operated during this cycle.
- Deleting a linked file in SharePoint or Google Drive, or removing MSCD's access to it. The control drops to Partially implemented once the waiting time has passed.
- Treating the pre-audit readiness check as the internal audit. The internal audit needs a programme, independent auditors and a report to management.
- Booking Stage 1 without a confirmed readiness checklist, or Stage 2 before one full internal audit and one management review are finished.
- Expecting the evidence pack to be the only thing the auditor looks at. The auditor picks the samples.
- Forgetting the mandatory records that have no screen yet, such as objectives, competence records, the internal audit programme, exercise results for ISO 22301 and gift, concern and investigation records for ISO 37001.
- Using the preparation task templates as they are, with the demo company's references in them.
- Looking for a 'stale' state. MSCD uses Aging, Expired and Critical for old evidence.

## Working through the clauses

This flow takes one clause or Annex A control from reading the requirement to the certification audit and the yearly surveillance visits, and shows which MSCD screen supports each step. A certification body samples this same path. It looks for a clear decision on whether the item applies, a named owner, a controlled document and current evidence that the control works. MSCD adds its own second-person check before a control counts as Implemented. The certification body also wants proof that the system checks itself through internal audit and management review in every year the certificate is held.

**When:** Use this flow once the management system exists and people hold roles in it. It runs from the first gap assessment to the certification decision, and again every year before each surveillance audit. It is written for ISO/IEC 27001 with its Annex A controls. ISO 9001, ISO 22301 and ISO 37001 follow the same path on their clause rows. They have no list of controls to select like ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A. The Annex A of ISO 9001 and of ISO 37001 is guidance only.

### Before you start

- The framework pack for the standard is installed and listed on Framework library. Packs are installed with 'Import bundle' on Framework library (on-premise) or with mscd-server packs install. For ISO 27001 this is ISO27001-2022. For ISO 37001 the current pack is ISO37001-2025, which is still a pre-release, and the ISO37001-2016 pack is marked withdrawn.
- A licence that includes the framework is installed. This is done with PUT /admin/license from the browser console.
- A Tenant Admin has created the management system with its scope statement (POST /admin/management-systems). After naming a Management System Manager, the Tenant Admin has moved it from 'setup' to 'implementing' (PATCH /admin/management-systems/{id}). Both steps are API only.
- Each person has been invited (POST /admin/users) and holds a business role in the system (POST /admin/management-systems/{id}/roles). A role grant to yourself or to an admin waits until a second person approves it (POST /admin/management-systems/{id}/roles/{roleId}/approve). A person with no role sees only the notice asking them to contact the Tenant Administrator.
- During customer onboarding, top management chose an accredited certification body, and the Management System Manager recorded the planned Stage 1 and Stage 2 audit events (POST /ms/{ms}/audit-events with eventType stage1 and stage2). This flow only confirms them.
- The organisation owns a licensed copy of the ISO standard. MSCD shows only clause numbers and short titles.
- A file repository is ready. The Managed Vault works right after installation. SharePoint or Google Drive repositories need setup through the API.
- For ISO 27001, risks are assessed and each has a chosen treatment in the risk register (/ms/{ms}/registers/risks and POST /ms/{ms}/risks/{id}/treatments, API only) before applicability is decided, so that every applicable control can be linked to the risks it treats. The first steps of the stage 'Read the requirement and decide applicability' cover this work.

### Decisions

- **Is the item one of clauses 4 to 10 of ISO 27001?** Yes: It must be met in full and cannot be excluded. MSCD does not block such an exclusion, so the Management System Manager must check that none is recorded. Go straight to assigning an owner. No: It is an Annex A control. Decide applicability for the Statement of Applicability first. ISO 9001 has no control list like Annex A. There, a requirement can be declared not applicable only if this does not affect product or service conformity or customer satisfaction, and the reason goes in the scope statement (ISO 9001:2015 clause 4.3). The common cases are 8.3 design and development and 7.1.5 measuring equipment. The ISO 9001 pack marks only clause 8 rows as open to this choice, and MSCD does not enforce that mark. In ISO 22301 and ISO 37001 every clause applies.
- **Is the Annex A control needed to treat a risk in scope, or required by law, contract or another obligation?** Yes: Mark it applicable, write the reason and link the risks it treats, which clears BR-02. No: Mark it 'Not applicable' and say exactly why it is not needed. Filler such as 'n/a' triggers the BR-03 warning, and an auditor will question it.
- **Does the Phase board activity name an output document code, such as SOA-01, POL-01 or PRC-05?** Yes: Create a document with that exact code in the Document register and link it to the system before 'Mark complete' (BR-40). Get the document Approved before the activity is verified, because MSCD only warns when it is not (BR-47). No: Mark it complete when the work is done.
- **Is there evidence that is Fresh or Aging, has an intact or moved link, and was produced in the current operating period?** Yes: The verifier can verify the control to 'Implemented'. No: Submit or re-link current evidence first. 'Verify' will be refused (BR-01).
- **For a control: did you mark it complete yourself, or, when nobody is recorded as completer, are you its owner?** Yes: Ask another Management System Manager or a Programme Owner to verify it. No: You can verify it if you hold the Management System Manager or Programme Owner role.
- **For a Phase board activity: did you complete it, or are you its owner?** Yes: Ask another Management System Manager or a Programme Owner to verify it. Only a Management System Manager can then verify the phase. No: You can verify it if you hold the Management System Manager or Programme Owner role.
- **Is every item on the Stage 1 readiness checklist finished, approved or planned, and is the Stage 1 document set ready for the auditor?** Yes: Give the auditor guest access and hold Stage 1 on the agreed date. No: Finish the missing items first, or agree a new Stage 1 date with the certification body and change the audit event with PATCH /ms/{ms}/audit-events/{id}.
- **Did Stage 1 show that the system is ready for Stage 2, with at least one internal audit covering every clause and every applicable control, and one management review using its results, complete and on record?** Yes: Confirm the Stage 2 date and prepare control owners for sampling. No: Fix the Stage 1 concerns and finish the internal audit and the management review first. Without them, the Stage 2 auditor normally raises a major nonconformity or stops the audit (ISO/IEC 17021-1 clause 9.3.1.2, and ISO/IEC 27006-1 for ISO 27001). The certification body may move the Stage 2 date.
- **Did the certification audit raise a major nonconformity?** Yes: Record the correction, the root cause analysis and the check for the same problem elsewhere, then verify that the fix worked (BR-06, BR-07, BR-08). The certification body sets a deadline and must accept the fix before it decides on certification. If it cannot verify the fix within 6 months of the last Stage 2 day, Stage 2 is repeated (ISO/IEC 17021-1 clause 9.5). No: For a minor nonconformity, agree a corrective action plan that the certification body accepts. An OFI closes with a note.
- **Did an internal audit raise a nonconformity?** Yes: The finding owner fixes it through a corrective action, a different person verifies the fix, and the Management System Manager closes the finding (clause 10.2). The certification body samples these records at its next visit. No: Record any OFI and close it with a note.
- **Is this the system's first certification audit?** Yes: Plan Stage 1 and Stage 2. Preparation tasks flagged for later audits are skipped (BR-44). No: Follow the stage 'Each surveillance year': a surveillance audit in years one and two, or the recertification audit in year three.
- **Was a surveillance audit missed, or is a major nonconformity still open after the certification body's deadline?** Yes: Contact the certification body at once and agree how to fix it. It can suspend the certificate until the problem is solved, and withdraw the certificate or reduce its scope if the problem stays. No: Keep the yearly rhythm: internal audit, management review, risk assessment review, document reviews and current evidence before each visit.

### Common mistakes

- Building the Statement of Applicability before the risk assessment and the treatment choices are done. An auditor reads that order as a sign that the SoA has no basis in risk.
- Treating 'Mark complete' as the finish line. A control counts as 'Implemented' only after a second person verifies it.
- Linking only the records produced while the control was being set up. Auditors sample records from the current cycle that show the control kept running.
- Uploading an old file and trusting its freshness status. The web app records today's date as the date the evidence was produced.
- Writing an exclusion reason such as 'n/a' or 'not relevant'. The reason has to explain why the control is not needed for the risks in scope.
- Trying to exclude one of clauses 4 to 10 in ISO 27001. Only Annex A controls can be left out, and MSCD will accept the exclusion without a warning.
- Expecting a risk treatment that names a control to clear the 'No risk-based justification' block. The risk must also be linked on the applicability entry.
- Reading the Phase board phases as ISO requirements. They are a suggested project plan, and the auditor checks the clauses and controls.
- Verifying a Phase board activity while its output document is still Draft. MSCD only warns, and the auditor will find an unapproved document.
- Expecting 'Reassign' on a document to change its owner. For a document, and for the reason task on a control's applicability, it changes only the person who must do the next step. For controls, evidence, activities, risks and preparation tasks it changes the owner.
- Counting the self-assessment as the internal audit. It is a readiness check, and clause 9.2 needs a planned audit by an independent auditor.
- Letting internal auditors audit their own controls. Internal auditors must be independent of the work they audit.
- Expecting top management to approve documents or record the management review in MSCD. A Management System Manager or Programme Owner records the decision, and the minutes are kept as evidence.
- Waiting for the phase 8 management review to approve the policy. Top management can approve it in an early leadership meeting, with the minutes kept as evidence.
- Choosing the certification body or creating the Stage 1 and Stage 2 events a second time. Both are done during customer onboarding, and this flow only confirms them.
- Booking Stage 1 before the first internal audit and management review are planned or done. Stage 1 checks for both.
- Sending the Stage 1 auditor to the guest screens to read documents. Guests cannot open document files or see risks, so prepare the Stage 1 document set.
- Holding Stage 2 before the first full internal audit and management review are finished.
- Expecting the certification auditor to record the result in MSCD. Guests can only read, so the Management System Manager records the outcome.
- Treating the certificate as the end of the work. The certification body returns every year, and a missed surveillance audit or an unfixed major nonconformity can lead to suspension.
- Leaving the yearly internal audit and management review until after the surveillance visit. Both should be finished before each visit.
- Reading 0 percent clause readiness as no progress. Readiness is never recalculated for real systems.
- Planning to approve a document you submitted yourself. BR-16 refuses this, so name a second approver early.
- Treating the exported SoA spreadsheet as the approved SoA. The approved version is the SOA-01 document in the register.

## Audit terms

### Core concepts

1. **Management system.** The set of policies, processes, roles and records an organisation uses to reach its goals in one area, such as information security or quality. An ISO standard says what the system must include. Certification means an independent certification body has audited the system and confirmed it meets the standard. An accredited certification body has itself been checked by a national accreditation body, such as KAN (Komite Akreditasi Nasional) in Indonesia. Example: PT Nusantara Digital Teknologi runs four: an ISMS (ISO/IEC 27001:2022) for information security, a QMS (ISO 9001) for quality, a BCMS (ISO 22301) for business continuity and an ABMS (ISO 37001) for anti-bribery. Rina runs the ISMS as ISMS Manager.
2. **Clause and requirement.** A clause is a numbered section of the standard. Clauses 4 to 10 hold the requirements, the things the organisation must do, and follow the same layout in ISO 27001, ISO 9001, ISO 22301 and ISO 37001. Auditors cite the clause number when they raise a finding. Example: Clause 9.2 covers internal audit and clause 9.3 management review. In the QMS, finding NC-2025-03 was raised against clause 8.4 (external providers) because two supplier re-evaluations were overdue.
3. **Context of the organisation and interested parties.** Context means the internal and external issues that affect what the management system must achieve. Interested parties are the people and groups whose needs the system must consider, such as customers, regulators, staff and suppliers, and the organisation decides which of their needs it will treat as requirements. Risks are drawn from both. Example: Context issue CI-03 'Ageing virtualisation platform in Surabaya' leads to risk R-031. CI-05 'Reliance on public cloud providers' leads to risk R-022. CI-01 'Multi-tenant hosting for regulated customers' points to customers and their regulators as interested parties.
4. **Scope.** The boundary of a management system: which sites, services, teams and systems it covers. The scope is decided after looking at the context issues and the needs of interested parties. The audit and the certificate cover only what is inside the scope, and the scope must be written down. Example: The ISMS scope is data centre and managed services operations in Jakarta and Surabaya. The BCMS covers only data centre operations in Jakarta.
5. **Top management, governing body and policy.** Top management is the person or group who directs and controls the organisation at the highest level. They must lead the system, set the policy, provide resources and take part in management review. The policy is their short written statement of what the system aims for and what the organisation commits to. ISO 37001 also names a governing body, such as a board of directors or, in an Indonesian company, the board of commissioners, which oversees top management on anti-bribery. Example: Hendra holds the Top Management role. He owns the Information Security Policy POL-01 and must approve the Statement of Applicability SOA-01. In the ABMS, recording the governing body's oversight is an open item due 15 Dec 2026, and the Anti-Bribery Policy POL-A01 is still a draft.
6. **Risks and opportunities: risk assessment and risk treatment.** Risk assessment finds what could go wrong, how likely it is and how bad it would be. Risk treatment decides what to do about each risk: reduce it with controls, accept it, avoid it or share it. The standards also ask the organisation to look for opportunities, things that could turn out better than planned. The method differs by standard. ISO/IEC 27001: risk assessment, risk treatment and an SoA. ISO 9001: actions on risks and opportunities, with no set method (6.1). ISO 22301: a business impact analysis and a risk assessment (8.2). ISO 37001: a bribery risk assessment (4.5). Example: R-031 'Unpatched hypervisor in Surabaya DC' has no treatment yet, so it blocks the ISMS 'Risk & SoA' checkpoint. In the QMS the same step is 'Risk & opportunity', and in the BCMS it starts with the business impact analysis.
7. **Control.** A measure that reduces a risk, such as a backup routine or an access review. For an ISMS, ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A lists 93 reference controls in four themes: organizational, people, physical and technological. The organisation chooses the ones its risks need and can add controls from other sources. The other standards set their requirements in their own clauses, such as the financial and non-financial controls in ISO 37001. Example: A.8.13 'Information backup' must show evidence every month. A.5.23 'Information security for use of cloud services' treats risk R-022.
8. **Statement of Applicability (SoA) and risk treatment plan.** Two separate ISMS documents from ISO/IEC 27001, and auditors ask for both. The SoA lists every Annex A control, says whether it applies, gives the reason for including or leaving it out, and shows whether it is in place. It also lists any extra controls chosen from other sources. The risk treatment plan says who will put which controls in place for each risk, and by when. Risk owners approve the plan and accept the risk that remains after treatment (residual risk). The SoA lists the controls; the plan says how and when they are delivered. Other management systems have no SoA. Example: SOA-01 'Statement of Applicability v3.0' is still a draft. Hendra was due to approve it by 15 Oct 2026, and until he does it blocks the 'Risk & SoA' checkpoint. Risk R-022 'Third-party cloud service outage' is treated by T-022 and control A.5.23, and is waiting for its risk owner to accept the residual risk.
9. **Objectives and measurement.** Objectives are measurable targets that follow from the policy, each with a plan that says what will be done, who acts and by when. Measurement means tracking results with set measures (metrics), so the organisation can show whether objectives are met and controls work. This is the 'measure' step at the end of each trace. Example: Quality objective QO-03 'Customer satisfaction at least 4.3 out of 5' is waiting for its Q3 measurement. In the ISMS, metric M-07 'Patch SLA compliance (%)' is missing its August and September values.
10. **Documented information (documents).** Information the organisation must control, such as policies and procedures. The standards ask that documents are reviewed and approved before use, that the right version is in use and available where needed, and that they are protected from loss or unwanted change. Most organisations also give each document an owner and a review date in their own procedure. For an ISMS, policies must also be reviewed at planned intervals. Example: POL-01 Information Security Policy v4.0 was approved on 10 Feb 2026 and is due for review on 10 Feb 2027. PRC-07 Access Control Procedure passed its review date on 24 Sep 2026, so its review is overdue under the organisation's own rule.
11. **Record and evidence.** A record is documented information kept to prove that something was done. Evidence is what an auditor checks to confirm a requirement is met; for a control it should come from the current period. In MSCD a second person verifies completed work against this evidence. Example: For A.8.13 the evidence is the 'Backup job log & restore test'. For A.6.3 (awareness training) it is the 'Awareness training attendance' record.
12. **Internal audit and management review.** An internal audit is an audit the organisation runs on itself at planned intervals, by people who do not audit their own work. It checks that the system meets the standard and the organisation's own rules, and its results go to management. Over the certification cycle, the internal audit programme should cover every clause, every applicable control and every site in scope. A management review is a planned meeting where top management looks at how the system performs, using set inputs such as audit results, risks, objectives and feedback, and records decisions on changes and resources. In practice both happen at least once a year, and the certification body checks them at every audit. Example: Maya, the Internal Auditor, ran the 2026 ISMS internal audit ahead of Surveillance audit 1 on 12 Nov 2026. The 2026 ISMS management review, led by Hendra, is due on 28 Oct 2026, and two of its seven inputs are not ready yet.
13. **Nonconformity and opportunity for improvement.** These are audit findings. A nonconformity (NC) is a requirement that is not met. A minor nonconformity means a requirement is not fully met, but the system can still deliver its results. A major nonconformity means the system cannot reliably deliver its results, for example because a required process is missing, or because there are many minor nonconformities on the same requirement. An opportunity for improvement (OFI) is a suggestion that needs no formal fix. Example: NC-2025-02 (minor, A.8.8) found that the deadline to fix 3 critical vulnerabilities was missed. OFI-2025-01 noted that metric definitions lack target thresholds.
14. **Corrective action.** The steps taken after a nonconformity: fix the immediate problem (correction), find the root cause, check for the same problem elsewhere, act so it does not come back, update the risk assessment or the management system if needed, then check later that the action worked (effectiveness). In MSCD a different person verifies the result. Example: For NC-2025-02 the corrective action is in place, and Bagus must close it with an effectiveness check due 20 Oct 2026. NC-2025-01 was closed on 6 Feb 2026 after its effectiveness was verified.
15. **Stage 1 and Stage 2 audit.** The certification body's first audit has two parts. Stage 1 checks that the system is designed and documented: the scope, the risk method and, for an ISMS, the SoA. It also checks that internal audits and management review are planned and under way. At least one full internal audit and one management review must be finished before Stage 2 can take place. Stage 1 has no pass or fail; it decides whether the organisation is ready for Stage 2. Stage 2 checks that the system works in practice through interviews and samples. The certification body then reviews the audit team's report and makes the certification decision. Before it decides, it must accept the action plans for minor nonconformities and confirm that any major nonconformity has been fixed. Example: PT Sertifikasi Nusantara, with lead auditor Ir. Dwi Hartanto, held the ISMS Stage 1 on 18 Aug 2025 (ready for Stage 2, 2 OFI) and Stage 2 on 3 Nov 2025 (2 minor NC, 1 OFI). After accepting the action plans for both minor NCs, it decided to certify the ISMS on 14 Nov 2025. The BCMS Stage 1 is planned for 10 Feb 2027, and its internal audit and management review must be finished before Stage 2 on 20 Apr 2027.
16. **Surveillance audit and recertification.** A certificate runs for a three-year cycle. The certification body holds the first surveillance audit within 12 months of the certification decision, then at least one in each calendar year until recertification. Each surveillance audit checks that the system still works, that internal audits and management review took place, and that earlier findings were fixed. In year three a recertification audit renews the certificate, and the certification body must make its decision before the old certificate ends; otherwise the certificate lapses. Special audits, such as for a scope extension, and transition audits to a new edition can happen in between. Example: The ISMS certificate runs from 14 Nov 2025 to 13 Nov 2028. Surveillance audit 1 is on 12 Nov 2026 (2 days), Surveillance 2 is in Nov 2027 and recertification is in Sep 2028. The QMS certificate runs from 28 Dec 2023 to 27 Dec 2026, so its recertification audit on 3 Dec 2026 leaves time for the decision before the end date.
