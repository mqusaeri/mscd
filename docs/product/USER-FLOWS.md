# MSCD user flows

Draft. The ISO lead-auditor review and the code accuracy check of every step are still to do. Milestone 2 (docs/platform/MILESTONE-2.md) builds the screens for the steps marked Partial or Not built.

Four user flows that show who does what in MSCD, in which screen, and why an auditor cares. Status per step: **Available** (works in the app), **Partial** (command line, API or with limits), **Not built**.

## The journey

1. **Onboard the customer.** Set up the organisation, sign-in, management systems and storage.
2. **Assign people.** Give each person a role and make them owner of their work.
3. **Prepare documents.** Write and approve the documents, collect the records that prove them.
4. **Work the clauses.** Go clause by clause and control by control until each one is verified.
5. **Certification audit.** Stage 1 checks the documents, stage 2 checks that you follow them.
6. **Stay certified.** Surveillance audits every year, recertification every three years.

## Customer onboarding

Takes a signed customer from an empty server, or a new SaaS organisation, to an organisation in MSCD. At the end, each licensed management system exists with its scope, edition, file storage and audit dates, and the core team can sign in with the right roles. This matters for certification because the scope, the role assignments and the access controls set up here are among the first things a certification auditor checks.

**When:** Use this flow when a customer has signed the contract and needs a working MSCD organisation. It covers everything up to the day the core team starts implementation work on the first management system.

### Who is involved

| Role | Shown in MSCD as | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Software supplier | CyberLentera | Delivers the signed offline bundle, its checksum file and the public key. Issues the licence file bound to the installation id. Supports the setup of SaaS operator accounts. |
| Customer IT administrator (server operator) | operator (runs install.sh and the cli container) | Prepares the server, certificate, email relay and firewall. Runs the installer, keeps the secrets folder safe, reads the installation id and runs the server commands such as verify-domain. |
| Hosting service operator (SaaS only) | Platform Operator | Creates SaaS organisations on the shared platform. Installs licences and framework packs at platform level. |
| Application administrator for the organisation | Tenant Admin (Tenant Administrator) | On-premise, installs the licence. Connects sign-in and storage, creates the management systems, invites people and grants their roles. The role gives no access to the business screens by itself. |
| Second application administrator (approver) | Tenant Admin | Approves or rejects role grants that the first admin may not activate alone, such as a grant to themselves or to another admin. This keeps duties separated. |
| Document storage administrator | Storage Admin | Connects and tests SharePoint Online or Google Shared Drive repositories, sets routing rules and watches link health. |
| Top management | Top Management | Approves the scope of each system. Names the people who carry responsibility and authority for each system. Signs with the certification body. In MSCD sees progress as totals. |
| Management system manager (the person given responsibility and authority for the system, often called the management representative) | MS Manager (one of them marked primary) | Runs one system day to day. Records the audit dates, loads the controls or clauses, assigns work and verifies what others complete. |
| Implementation programme lead (project sponsor) | Programme Owner (shown in the app as MS Manager) | Leads the certification project across a system. Can verify, approve documents and see everyone's pending work. |
| Control owner or process owner | Domain Owner | Owns controls, documents and evidence in one area and works mainly from My Tasks. |
| Risk owner | Risk Owner (shown in the app as Domain Owner) | Owns risks, updates their treatment and accepts the remaining risk on their own risks. |
| Internal auditor | Internal Auditor | Checks the system independently before the certification audit. Should be a different person from the MS Manager of the same system. |
| Certification body and its lead auditor | Certification body, lead auditor (later External Auditor with guest access) | Agrees the Stage 1 and Stage 2 audit dates. Gets time-limited, read-only guest access later through Auditor access. |

### Before you start

- A signed contract and a licence order with CyberLentera that lists the frameworks, the number of users (active members without guests), the number of management systems, the storage adapters (m365, google_drive) and the features needed (for example auditor_guest_access, link_health_monitor, edition_transition).
- A server: 64-bit Linux on x86-64 with Docker Engine 24 or later, the Docker Compose plugin 2.20 or later, openssl, sha256sum, tar, curl and sudo access. For production up to 150 users: 8 vCPU, 32 GB RAM, 500 GB SSD. For Kubernetes: version 1.29 or later, Helm 3, an internal registry, an ingress controller, PostgreSQL 15 or later and an S3-compatible store.
- A host name in DNS and a TLS certificate and key from the company certificate authority. A self-signed certificate works for a trial, but every browser shows a warning.
- Email relay details for invitations and notifications: SMTP host, port, account, password file and sender address.
- Firewall openings from the server to Microsoft and Google over HTTPS when Entra ID, SharePoint Online or Google Drive will be used.
- The final organisation short name (slug: a lower-case letter followed by 2 to 40 lower-case letters, digits or underscores) and the display name. The slug can never change.
- Work emails of two people who will be Tenant Admins.
- An approved scope statement for each management system (up to 4000 characters), signed by top management.
- The chosen certification body, the lead auditor name if known, and target dates for the Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits.
- A role list per management system: primary MS Manager, Programme Owner, Domain Owners, Risk Owners, Internal Auditor and Top Management viewers.
- For single sign-on: an Entra ID (or other OIDC or SAML) administrator who can register apps, and the company email domain if accounts should be created automatically.
- For customer file storage: a SharePoint site with an admin who can grant the Sites.Selected permission, or a Google Shared Drive with a service account, and one folder per management system.
- A safe place, such as the company password safe, for the copy of the secrets folder and the recovery codes.

### Decisions

- **Will MSCD run on the customer's own server with Docker Compose?** Yes: Run install.sh. It creates the organisation and can print the first admin link. No: On Kubernetes, install with Helm and create the organisation and first admin with kubectl exec. On SaaS, the Platform Operator creates the organisation with tenant create.
- **Does the organisation work outside the Asia/Jakarta time zone or across several sites?** Yes: The Compose installer cannot set either value and nothing changes them later. Raise it with CyberLentera before installing. On Kubernetes or SaaS, pass --time-zone and --site-count to tenant create. No: Keep the installer defaults.
- **Will there be two Tenant Admins from the start?** Yes: The second admin approves any business role the first admin gives to themselves or to another admin. No: Give the primary MS Manager role to a person who holds no admin role, because that grant is active at once. A lone Tenant Admin cannot activate a business role for themselves.
- **Does the customer use Microsoft Entra ID or another OIDC or SAML identity provider?** Yes: Connect and test it, invite the people, then set the local sign-in policy to admins_only. No: Keep local sign-in allowed. Every person uses a password and an authenticator app. Direct LDAP sign-in is not available.
- **Should accounts be created automatically at the first SSO sign-in?** Yes: Use an OIDC provider that sends verified emails, have the operator verify the email domain, and turn on jitProvisioning. Roles are still granted by hand. No: Invite every person with POST /admin/users.
- **Will documents and evidence stay in SharePoint Online or a Google Shared Drive?** Yes: Check that the licence includes m365 or google_drive. Create the systems first, then connect the repository with one root per system and point each system at it. No: Use the Managed Vault, which is ready right after installation.
- **Is an anti-bribery management system (ISO 37001) part of the order?** Yes: Use the ISO37001-2025 beta pack and confirm with the certification body which edition they will audit against. No: No action needed.
- **Will the quality management system be certified to ISO 9001:2026?** Yes: Choose pack ISO9001-2026 with edition 2026. Its content still follows the 2015 edition for now. No: Choose ISO9001-2015 and plan the move to the new edition before the transition end date of 2029-09-15.
- **Are the Stage 1 and Stage 2 dates agreed with the certification body?** Yes: The MS Manager records both audit events right after the roles are granted. No: Audit readiness shows 'No audit event scheduled' until the dates are recorded.

### What you have at the end

- A running MSCD installation from a checked bundle, with TLS, email relay and a safe copy of the secrets folder.
- An organisation with its final slug, its Managed Vault and the audit trail entry tenant.created.
- Two Tenant Admins, each with a password, an authenticator app and saved recovery codes.
- A valid licence bound to the installation id.
- The chosen sign-in method: a tested identity provider and a local sign-in policy, or local sign-in with two-step codes for everyone.
- One management system per licensed standard with its edition, approved scope statement, certification body and default repository, in status implementing.
- Connected and tested repositories with one root folder per system, when customer storage is used.
- The core team invited, with roles granted per system and one primary MS Manager per system.
- Stage 1 and Stage 2 audit events on the Certification timeline.
- Audit trail entries for every administrative change made during setup.

### Common mistakes

- Picking the organisation slug casually. It can never change, and a second install with another slug is refused.
- Installing with the defaults for an organisation outside the Jakarta time zone. Nightly checks, reminders and the 07:00 digest then run on Jakarta time.
- Forgetting to copy the secrets folder. Without it the database and the encrypted files cannot be opened after a failure.
- Expecting the Tenant Admin to see the business screens. The admin role carries no business permission, so the admin sees only the 'no role' notice until a business role is active.
- Starting with a single Tenant Admin who is also meant to be the MS Manager. The self-grant stays pending until a second Tenant Admin approves it.
- Trying to invite the team before the licence is installed. Every invitation after the first admin fails with LIC-USERS.
- Creating an anti-bribery system on ISO 37001:2016. That edition is withdrawn in MSCD and the request is refused.
- Connecting a repository before the systems exist, or keying its roots by the upper-case code. The server expects the slug of an existing system.
- Assuming SSO matches people by email. Every person still needs an invitation or a pre-registered identity.
- Setting local sign-in to disabled before SSO has been tested with a second account, which can lock everyone out.
- Treating the scope text in MSCD as the controlled scope document. It has no version or approval, so the signed scope must also live in the Document register.
- Giving the MS Manager and the Internal Auditor role of one system to the same person, which breaks the independence the internal audit needs.
- Letting invitation links expire. They are valid for 72 hours only.
- Comparing a fresh customer with the demo. A new system starts with no controls, no cycle checklist and no traceability chains.

## User and role assignment

This flow gives each person the access and the responsibility their job needs in each management system, and takes it away when they no longer need it. Certification auditors check three things: top management has assigned the roles, every risk, control, document and corrective action has a named owner and a due date, and access is limited, checked by a second person and reviewed.

**When:** Use it when a management system is first set up, when someone joins, changes job or leaves, when a piece of work needs a new owner or a new date, and in the weeks before a certification audit, when the external auditor needs access.

### Who is involved

| Role | Shown in MSCD as | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Top management | Top Management (business role top_management) | The person or group who directs the organisation at the highest level. Decides who is responsible for each management system and makes sure people know. In MSCD they read progress, see other people's pending work as totals only, and cannot open stored files. |
| Management system manager (older texts say management representative; ISO 37001 calls it the anti-bribery compliance function) | MS Manager (business role ms_manager; pack titles ISMS Manager, QMS Manager, BCMS Manager, Anti-Bribery Compliance Function). One holder per system can be marked primary. | Runs one management system day to day. Assigns and reassigns owners and due dates, verifies work others complete, sends reminders, sees everyone's pending work, manages findings and corrective actions, and gives the external auditor guest access. The primary MS Manager is shown as the manager of the system and owns the audit preparation tasks the platform creates. |
| Certification programme sponsor (no ISO term; usually a senior manager) | Programme Owner (business role programme_owner; the screens show this person as 'MS Manager') | Oversees the certification programme. Can verify work, approve documents, reassign items, send reminders and see pending work per person. Cannot create or edit records. |
| Control owner or process owner | Domain Owner (business role domain_owner) | Accountable for specific controls, documents, evidence or processes. Sees only the records they own or hold. Completes the work, submits evidence and reviews their documents, mainly from My Tasks. |
| Risk owner | Risk Owner (business role risk_owner; the screens show 'Domain Owner') | Accountable for a risk. Updates its assessment and treatment, approves the treatment plan and accepts the remaining (residual) risk for the risks they own. |
| Investigator of raised concerns (ISO 37001) | Compliance Investigator (business role compliance_investigator; the screens show 'Domain Owner') | Handles concerns and investigations in the anti-bribery system. Reads and completes only their own items. |
| Internal auditor | Internal Auditor (business role internal_auditor) | Checks, on behalf of the organisation, that the management system works. Reads the whole system, raises findings, verifies corrective actions, builds evidence packs and sends reminders. Should not audit work they own. |
| External certification auditor (auditor from the certification body) | External Auditor (business role external_auditor), created as a guest through 'Auditor access'. The role menu shows 'Internal Auditor'. | Audits the system for certification. Has read-only access to one system for a limited time, and sees files only inside ready evidence packs. |
| System administrator of the organisation (privileged user) | Tenant Admin (also 'Tenant Administrator' on sign-in pages; organisation role tenant_admin) | Invites people, gives and removes admin roles and business roles, disables leavers, resets second factors, and sets up sign-in. | Storage administrator | Storage Admin (organisation role storage_admin) | Connects and tests file repositories, sets file routing and runs link health checks. Holds no business permission. |
| Hosting operator (IT team running the server, or the SaaS provider) | Platform Operator, and the operator who runs install.sh and the CLI | Installs MSCD, creates the organisation and the first Tenant Admin from the command line, and can reset any account's second factor. |

### Before you start

- MSCD is installed, the organisation exists, and the first Tenant Admin has accepted the invitation, set a password and added an authenticator app.
- A licence is installed with enough user seats (otherwise inviting fails with LIC-USERS). Guest auditor access needs the licence feature auditor_guest_access.
- Each management system to be staffed has been created (POST /admin/management-systems). A new system starts in status 'setup'.
- Outgoing email (SMTP) is set with the installer options, because invitation links, reminders and guest links arrive by email.
- Top management has decided, in writing, who holds which responsibility in each system. MSCD has no place to record this decision.
- The Tenant Admin can use the browser console helper mscd(method, path, body) from docs/INSTALL-ONPREM.md section 7, since most people administration has no screen.
- For single sign-on users, the identity provider (for example Microsoft Entra ID) is connected.

### Decisions

- **Has the person accepted the invitation yet?** Yes: Grant business roles now. No: Wait. A business role can only be given to an active member, so the grant is refused with 'Name a member'.
- **Does the person already have an MSCD account in another organisation?** Yes: Inviting adds a membership. The person accepts it with POST /me/memberships/{org}/accept after signing in. The Tenant Admin cannot change their name, account status or second factor. No: The person gets an email link valid for 72 hours and joins on the 'Join MSCD' page.
- **Will the person sign in with company single sign-on?** Yes: Register their provider identity with POST /admin/users/{id}/identities, or let them link it after a first password sign-in. No: They use a password and an authenticator app.
- **Is the role grant for yourself, or for someone who is Tenant Admin or Storage Admin?** Yes: The grant waits for approval by a second Tenant Admin. Make sure one exists. No: The grant is active at once.
- **Is this person the main manager of the system?** Yes: Grant MS Manager with isPrimary true. If another person holds the primary grant, revoke that first. No: Grant MS Manager without the primary mark.
- **Does the internal auditor own any of the work they will audit?** Yes: Choose another internal auditor for that area. The platform will not warn you. No: Grant Internal Auditor.
- **Is the item a finding or a corrective action?** Yes: Change its owner and due date with PATCH from the API (MS Manager only). No: Use 'Reassign' or 'Change due date' on the screen.
- **Does the external auditor already have a regular MSCD account?** Yes: The Tenant Admin adds a membership and an External Auditor role with an end date through the API. No: Use 'Auditor access' on Audit readiness to create a guest login.
- **Does the audit window need more or fewer than 14 days of access?** Yes: Create the grant through the API with expiresInDays from 1 to 30. No: Use the 'Auditor access' button.
- **Does the leaver still hold open items?** Yes: Reassign every item first, then revoke roles and disable the membership. No: Revoke roles and disable the membership.
- **Does the leaver belong to another MSCD organisation?** Yes: Disable only the membership in this organisation. No: Disable the membership and the account.

### What you have at the end

- Active member accounts, each with a password and an authenticator app or a registered single sign-on identity.
- At least two Tenant Admins, and a Storage Admin where needed.
- Business role grants per management system, with one primary MS Manager each, and approval records for grants checked by a second Tenant Admin.
- A named owner and a due date on every open control, document step, evidence item, activity, risk, finding and corrective action.
- Reminder records and audit trail entries (user.invited, record.assigned, reminder.sent, auditor.read, auditor_access.revoked).
- Time-limited, read-only guest access for the certification auditor, plus a ready evidence pack.
- Revoked roles and disabled memberships for people who left, with their open work handed over.
- Kept outside the platform today: top management's written assignment of roles and the periodic access review record.

### Common mistakes

- Making someone Tenant Admin and expecting them to see the screens. Admin roles carry no business permission, so the person sees only the 'You hold no role' notice until they get a business role.
- Running the organisation with one Tenant Admin. Grants to yourself or to other admins then stay pending for ever.
- Granting a business role before the person has accepted the invitation. Only active members can receive a role.
- Disabling a leaver before handing over their work. Nothing moves their items, and reminders to them are refused.
- Reassigning an item and assuming the new owner knows. Only preparation tasks send a notice, so send a reminder right after.
- Choosing a new holder who has no role in that system. The server accepts it, but My Tasks lists items only from systems where the person holds a role, so the item can stay out of their sight.
- Reading 'Holder' and 'Owner' as different people on every screen. For controls, evidence, activities and risks Reassign changes the owner. For a document it changes who must do the next step, and the document owner stays the same.
- Reading 'Internal Auditor' in the role menu as proof the person is internal. External auditors show the same label. A Programme Owner shows as 'MS Manager', and Risk Owners and Compliance Investigators show as 'Domain Owner'.
- Letting the internal auditor also own controls or risks in the area they audit. Auditors expect internal auditors to be independent of the work they check.
- Planning one person per system. The person who completes an item cannot verify it, so each system needs a second person with verify rights.
- Giving the external auditor access before building the evidence pack. The guest sees files only inside ready packs.
- Forgetting that guest access from the button always ends after 14 days and cannot be extended on screen. Plan the start date around the audit dates.
- Sending a second reminder about the same item to the same person within 72 hours. It is refused.

## Document preparation and prerequisites

Prepare the documented information an ISO audit expects: the documents that say how the organisation works and the records that prove it worked. Then clear the prerequisites (gate items, phase sign-offs and once-per-cycle items) that must be met before a phase is signed off or the certification audit can pass. Auditors judge a management system mostly by reading these documents and sampling these records, so a gap here usually becomes an audit finding.

**When:** Use this flow when a new management system starts implementation, when a Phase board activity names a required output document, when a document reaches its review date, when evidence turns Aging or Expired, and during the 60 days before a Stage 1, Stage 2 or surveillance audit.

### Who is involved

| Role | Shown in MSCD as | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Top management | Top Management | Sets the direction of the management system, endorses the top-level policy and objectives, and takes part in the management review. In MSCD this role can only read and cannot approve documents. |
| Management system manager (the person given responsibility and authority for the ISMS) | MS Manager (shown with a per-system title such as ISMS Manager) | Runs the document register: creates entries, sets owners, approvers and review dates, links documents to controls, verifies completed work and signs off phases. Can approve documents that someone else submitted. |
| Management system sponsor (programme owner) | Programme Owner (the app shows this person as MS Manager) | Can approve documents and verify completed work. Useful as the second person who approves what the MS Manager wrote. |
| Document owner (author) | Owner column in the Document register, usually a Domain Owner | Writes and updates the document, submits each new version for review, and starts the review when the review date comes. |
| Document approver (authorised approver) | Approver field on the document | Checks that a version is correct and fit for use, then approves it or returns it with a note. Must hold MS Manager or Programme Owner and must be a different person from the one who submitted the version. |
| Process or control owner (record owner) | Owner on controls and evidence, usually a Domain Owner | Runs the control day to day and submits the records that prove it ran, on time for its operating frequency. |
| Risk owner | Domain Owner (business role risk_owner) | Approves the risk treatment plan for their risks and accepts the remaining risk. Has no document review rights in MSCD. |
| Internal auditor | Internal Auditor | Checks before the audit that required documents are approved and records are current, raises findings, and builds the evidence pack. |
| Certification body auditor (external auditor) | Guest created with 'Auditor access'; the app shows the label Internal Auditor | Reads documents and samples records during Stage 1 and Stage 2. Has read-only access and sees files only inside ready evidence packs. |
| Storage administrator | Storage Admin or Tenant Admin | Connects and maintains the file repository (Managed Vault, SharePoint or Google Drive) where document and evidence files are kept. |

### Before you start

- A management system exists for the standard and edition, for example ISO/IEC 27001:2022, and is in status implementing. Today this is set up through the API (POST /admin/management-systems).
- Each person in this flow holds a business role in that system: an MS Manager, a Programme Owner or second MS Manager so that the author and the approver can be different people, and the document and evidence owners.
- A file repository is ready. Managed Vault works right after installation. A SharePoint or Google Drive repository needs a root folder per system with a Documents folder and Evidence/<year>/Q<quarter> folders, because the file picker lists only those.
- The scope of the management system is decided and written in the system's scope statement.
- Control entries exist for the system, each with an applicable yes or no answer and a reason (the Statement of Applicability decisions). A new system starts with none, and each one is added through the API. These decisions tell you which control documents and records you need.
- The operating frequency of each applicable control is set, so the platform knows how often new evidence is due.
- The team agrees a numbering scheme that uses the exact codes the framework pack expects for activity outputs: POL-01, PRC-05, SOA-01, PRC-07, PRC-08, PRC-11, PRC-13 and IMS-01 to IMS-04 for ISO 27001.

### Decisions

- **Does this item say how the organisation works, such as a policy, procedure or plan?** Yes: Treat it as a document: give it a register entry with tier, owner, approver and review date, and send each version through review and approval. No: Treat it as a record: submit it as evidence against the control it proves. It is kept as produced and judged by its age.
- **Does a Phase board activity name an output code for this document?** Yes: Create the register entry with that exact code before marking the activity complete (BR-40). No: Use a code from your own numbering scheme and keep the activity's result as evidence.
- **Is the document used by more than one management system?** Yes: Register one organisation-wide copy and link it to every system. Expect a confirmation step on each change (BR-22). No: Link it only to the system it serves.
- **Is the Annex A control applicable in the Statement of Applicability?** Yes: It needs an implementing document, a linked risk (ISO 27001 and ISO 37001) and current evidence before it can be verified. No: Write a real reason for leaving it out. Fillers such as 'n/a' give a warning. No document or evidence is needed for it.
- **Are the files kept in SharePoint or Google Drive?** Yes: Use link mode with 'Link selected file', and leave the file where it is afterwards. Moving or renaming it shows the link as Moved or Missing. No: Upload to Managed Vault, which keeps versions and a SHA-256 fingerprint.
- **Has the document passed its review date?** Yes: It is Expired. Press 'Start review', update or confirm the content, and get it approved by someone other than the submitter. No: Plan the review in the Review calendar and act on the alerts at 60, 30, 14 and 7 days.
- **Does the content of an approved document need to change?** Yes: Use 'Upload new version'. The new version goes to In review and needs approval by someone other than the submitter. No: Keep the current version. At the review date, approve the review with no change.
- **Is the evidence Fresh or Aging, produced in the current period, with a verified file?** Yes: The control can be verified to Implemented. No: Submit or re-link a current file. Until then the control stays at Partial.
- **Is a phase activity stuck because of a gate item?** Yes: Record a blocker that names the gate item and a reason, then fix the gate item. No: Complete the activity and ask a different person to verify its output.

### What you have at the end

- A document register in which every required document is Approved, with its owner, approver, current version and next review date.
- A stored version trail per document: each version with who submitted it, who approved it and when (not yet viewable on screen).
- Shared organisation-wide procedures (IMS-01 to IMS-04) linked to every system that uses them.
- Each applicable control linked to its implementing document and to at least one current evidence record.
- Evidence files with pinned versions and fingerprints, each with a freshness state of Fresh or Aging.
- Verified Phase board activities and a verified critical gate phase (Risk & SoA for ISO 27001).
- A completed audit preparation checklist and an evidence pack with its manifest and list of left-out items.
- Exported Statement of Applicability and document register files (XLSX) for the auditor.

### Common mistakes

- Showing the auditor a procedure as proof that a control works. The auditor will also ask for records from the current cycle.
- Submitting last month's or last year's records through the screen, which stamps them with today's date and makes them look fresh.
- Planning for the author to approve their own document. MSCD refuses this (BR-16), so name a second approver from the start.
- Using a document code that differs from the Phase board output code, so the activity cannot be marked complete.
- Letting review dates pass. A document still in use after its review date is a common audit finding.
- Keeping the scope only in the system's scope text, with no controlled scope document behind it.
- Writing 'n/a' or 'not relevant' as the reason for leaving out an Annex A control.
- Linking evidence produced at go-live. The auditor wants proof that the control operated during this cycle.
- Moving or renaming a linked file in SharePoint or Google Drive, which breaks its link status.
- Forgetting the mandatory records that have no screen yet, such as objectives, competence records and the internal audit programme.
- Using the preparation task templates as they are, with the demo company's references in them.
- Looking for a 'stale' state. MSCD uses Aging, Expired and Critical for old evidence.

## Working through the clauses

This flow takes one clause or Annex A control from reading the requirement to the certification audit, and shows which MSCD screen supports each step. A certification body samples this same path. It looks for a clear decision on whether the item applies, a named owner, a controlled document, current evidence and a second-person check. It also wants proof that the system checks itself through internal audit and management review.

**When:** Use this flow once the management system exists and people hold roles in it. It runs from the first gap assessment to the certification decision, and again every year before each surveillance audit. It is written for ISO/IEC 27001 with its Annex A controls. ISO 9001, ISO 22301 and ISO 37001 follow the same path on their clause rows and have no Annex A list.

| Role | Shown in MSCD as | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Top management (the people who direct the organisation at the highest level) | Top Management | Shows leadership, approves the policy and the scope, provides resources, chairs the management review and is interviewed in Stage 2. In MSCD this role sees other people's pending work as totals only. |
| Management system manager (the person top management makes responsible for the ISMS under clause 5.3) | MS Manager (titled ISMS Manager, QMS Manager, BCMS Manager or Anti-Bribery Compliance Function per system) | Runs the system day to day. ### Decisions

- **Is the item one of clauses 4 to 10 of ISO 27001?** Yes: It must be met in full and cannot be excluded. Go straight to assigning an owner. No: It is an Annex A control. Decide applicability for the Statement of Applicability first. In the ISO 9001 pack, only clause 8 rows can be declared not applicable, and only with a reason.
- **Is the Annex A control needed to treat a risk in scope, or required by law, contract or another obligation?** Yes: Mark it applicable, write the reason and link the risks it treats, which clears BR-02. No: Mark it 'Not applicable' and say exactly why it is not needed. Filler such as 'n/a' triggers the BR-03 warning, and an auditor will question it.
- **Does the Phase board activity name an output document code, such as SOA-01, POL-01 or PRC-05?** Yes: Create a document with that exact code in the Document register and link it to the system before 'Mark complete' (BR-40). No: Mark it complete when the work is done.
- **Is there evidence that is Fresh or Aging, has an intact or moved link, and was produced in the current operating period?** Yes: The verifier can verify the control to 'Implemented'. No: Submit or re-link current evidence first. 'Verify' will be refused (BR-01).
- **Did you complete the item yourself, or are you its owner?** Yes: Ask another MS Manager or Programme Owner to verify it. No: You can verify it if you hold the MS Manager or Programme Owner role.
- **Did Stage 1 show that the system is ready for Stage 2?** Yes: Confirm the Stage 2 date and prepare control owners for sampling. No: Fix the Stage 1 concerns first. The certification body may move the Stage 2 date.
- **Did an audit raise a major nonconformity?** Yes: Record the correction, the root cause analysis and the check for the same problem elsewhere, then verify that the fix worked (BR-06, BR-07, BR-08). The certification body must accept the fix before it decides on certification. No: For a minor nonconformity, agree a corrective action plan that the certification body accepts. An OFI closes with a note.
- **Is this the system's first certification audit?** Yes: Plan Stage 1 and Stage 2. Preparation tasks flagged for later audits are skipped (BR-44). No: Plan a surveillance audit in years one and two, or the recertification audit in year three.

### Common mistakes

- Treating 'Mark complete' as the finish line. A control counts as 'Implemented' only after a second person verifies it.
- Linking only the records produced while the control was being set up. Auditors sample records from the current cycle that show the control kept running.
- Uploading an old file and trusting its freshness status. The web app records today's date as the date the evidence was produced.
- Writing an exclusion reason such as 'n/a' or 'not relevant'. The reason has to explain why the control is not needed for the risks in scope.
- Trying to exclude one of clauses 4 to 10 in ISO 27001. Only Annex A controls can be left out.
- Reading the Phase board phases as ISO requirements. They are a suggested project plan, and the auditor checks the clauses and controls.
- Mixing up Owner and Holder. The owner stays accountable for the item, and the holder is whoever has the next action right now.
- Letting internal auditors audit their own controls. Internal auditors must be independent of the work they audit.
- Booking Stage 1 before the first internal audit and management review are planned or done. Stage 1 checks for both.
- Expecting the certification auditor to record the result in MSCD. Guests can only read, so the MS Manager records the outcome.
- Reading 0 percent clause readiness as no progress. Readiness is never recalculated for real systems.
- Planning to approve a document you submitted yourself. BR-16 refuses this, so name a second approver early.
- Treating the exported SoA spreadsheet as the approved SoA. The approved version is the SOA-01 document in the register.
